# Scene Match Pedagogical Strategy & Tiered Visual Scope

This document outlines COSYlanguages' pedagogical framework for visual interactive activities across CEFR proficiency levels (A0 through C2).

## Executive Summary & Core Principle

Scene-matching activities—where learners select vocabulary from a word bank and click or touch target hotspots within an vector-illustrated spatial environment—rely on **direct visual representation of concrete physical objects**.

Forcing abstract vocabulary into hotspot scene matching leads to contrived, artificial visual metaphors or degrades the format into isolated picture flashcards. To preserve educational integrity, visual activities in COSYlanguages follow a **tiered progression strategy**.

---

## Tiered Scope Matrix

| Level Range | Pedagogical Focus | Suitable Visual Format | Applicable Domains |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tier 1: A0–A2** | Concrete physical nouns, immediate physical environment | **Full Scene Match** (SVG room & environment hotspots) | Housing, rooms, furniture, clothing, body parts, food, city streets, vehicles, farm animals, weather & seasons, daily routines. |
| **Tier 2: B1** | Mixed concrete & domain-specific workflows | **Selective Scene Match** (Task-based physical environments) | Health & medical equipment, DIY workshop tools, culinary arts & restaurant kitchens, airport transit/travel terminals. |
| **Tier 3: B2–C2** | Abstract concepts, discourse structures, systems | **System Maps & Collocation Webs** (Non-scene visual diagrams) | Law & jurisprudence, fiscal policy & macroeconomics, AI & cybernetics, epistemology, diplomatic rhetoric, bioethics. |

---

## Tier Breakdown & Implementation Guidelines

### Tier 1: A0/A1 ➔ A2 (Full Scene Match)
* **Goal**: Build initial core vocabulary by pointing directly at the physical world.
* **Format**: 2D vector room/environment SVGs with 8–12 labeled object hotspots and inter-room door navigation walkthroughs.
* **Active Scenes**:
  1. *Living Room / Apartment* (`apartment`)
  2. *Bedroom & Furniture* (`bedroom`)
  3. *Kitchen & Cooking Utensils* (`kitchen`)
  4. *Bathroom & Personal Care* (`bathroom`)
  5. *Daily Routine & Time* (`routine`)
  6. *Four Seasons & Weather* (`seasons`)
  7. *Wardrobe & Clothing* (`clothing`)
  8. *Farm Animals & Nature* (`animals`)
  9. *City Street & Buildings* (`city`)

### Tier 2: B1 (Selective Specialized Scenes)
* **Goal**: Reinforce specialized vocabulary within physical context-rich environments.
* **Format**: Specialized scene matching focused purely on concrete sub-domains where physical objects map 1:1 with target terms.
* **Target Sub-domains**:
  - Medical/Hospital Ward (scalpel, stethoscope, IV drip, monitor)
  - DIY Hardware Workshop (lathe, calliper, solder, drill)
  - Professional Restaurant Kitchen (whisk, ladle, colander, convection oven)
  - Airport Transit Gate (boarding gate, conveyor belt, scanner, runway)
* **Exclusions**: Abstract B1 domains such as personal finance, psychology, or climate policy are excluded from scene matching.

### Tier 3: B2 through C2 (Alternative Visual Formats)
* **Goal**: Master abstract relationships, collocations, legal/institutional structures, and conceptual arguments.
* **Format**: Dedicated non-scene visual tools (Concept Maps, Interactive Collocation Webs, Process & Flow Diagrams, Rhetorical Tree Maps).
* **Rationale**: Abstract terms like *epistemology*, *constitutional litigation*, *fiscal policy*, or *game theory* cannot be hotspot-mapped onto 2D object scenes without producing contrived visual representations. They warrant custom-designed interactive visual diagrams rather than scene matching.
