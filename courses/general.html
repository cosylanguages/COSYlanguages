🧭 Course Selection Compass: Is This Course Necessary For You?

✅ This course is necessary if:

  • You want a robust, long-term foundation covering syntax, vocabulary, phonetics, reading, and formal writing without skipping essential grammar mechanics.
  • You want to systematically move up official CEFR milestones (A1 → A2 → B1 → B2 → C1 → C2) for personal accomplishment or international relocation.
  • You prefer having structured homework, written feedback, and guided communicative speaking drills in every single lesson.

⚠️ Otherwise, choose another course:

  • Choose the Spoken Course if you already grasp basic grammar rules and solely want spontaneous conversational practice in 15 or 30-minute sessions without homework worksheets.
  • Choose the Exam Preparation Course if you need an official certificate (IELTS, TOEFL, Cambridge, DELF/DALF, CILS, CELI, TORFL) within the next 1–6 months and need targeted rubric drills.
  • Choose the Professional Course if you already have intermediate fluency (B1+) and need workplace-specific language for IT, Academia, Hospitality, or Legal affairs.
  • Choose the Relocation Course if your immediate priority is municipal administration, apartment leases, and healthcare setup.

🗺️ The Complete CEFR Level Roadmap (A1 to C2)

Our curriculum establishes exact grammatical inventories, thematic vocabulary domains, cognitive demands, and demonstrable can-do skills at every CEFR milestone:

🌱 Level A1: Breakthrough & Discovery 0 – 100 Guided Hours

Pedagogical Goal: Overcome fear of speaking from lesson one. Establish clear phonetic habits and formulate basic affirmative, negative, and interrogative sentences for concrete daily needs.

🧩 Grammar Inventory

  • Present simple tense & essential auxiliary verbs (to be, to have)
  • Definite and indefinite articles, gender and plural noun inflections
  • Subject, possessive, and demonstrative pronouns
  • Basic word order (SVO) and forming WH- / inversion questions
  • Cardinal numbers, dates, telling time, and common prepositions of place

📚 Vocabulary Themes (~600–800 words)

  • Self-introductions, nationality, languages, professions
  • Immediate family, home furnishings, colors, clothing
  • Basic food, ordering in a café, daily routines, days & months
  • Numbers 0–1,000, physical descriptions, simple weather

⚡ Demands & Expectations

  • 1–2 hours of self-study between sessions
  • Active repetition of target phonetics to avoid persistent accent traps
  • Speech rate: slow, clear, supported by gestures or repetition if necessary

🎯 Can-Do Abilities & Knowledge

  • Fill out simple forms with personal details (hotel check-in, address)
  • Ask and answer basic questions about possessions and where people live
  • Understand simple audio announcements when spoken slowly and clearly
🌿 Level A2: Waystage & Survival 100 – 200 Guided Hours

Pedagogical Goal: Communicate in simple, routine tasks requiring a direct exchange of information on familiar matters. Narrate recent experiences in the past and project future plans.

🧩 Grammar Inventory

  • Past tenses (Simple Past / Passé Composé / Passato Prossimo / Прошедшее)
  • Future expressions (going to, will, future simple)
  • Modal verbs of obligation, permission, and ability (can, must, should)
  • Comparative and superlative degrees of adjectives
  • Direct and indirect object pronouns; basic time prepositions

📚 Vocabulary Themes (~1,200–1,600 words)

  • Shopping, transactions, prices, clothes shopping, markets
  • Local geography, city navigation, public transport directions
  • Past vacations, childhood memories, hobbies and personal interests
  • Health symptoms, pharmacy visits, simple medical advice

⚡ Demands & Expectations

  • Consistent short writing assignments (informal postcards, short emails)
  • Ability to sustain 2–3 minute spoken monologues on personal topics
  • Comprehension of short, simple printed texts and brochures

🎯 Can-Do Abilities & Knowledge

  • Describe in simple terms aspects of background, environment, and matters of immediate need
  • Participate in short social exchanges without immediate translation support
  • Extract key details from menus, timetables, advertisements, and signs
🌾 Level B1: Threshold & Independence 200 – 400 Guided Hours

Pedagogical Goal: Enter unprepared into conversations on familiar topics. Express opinions, reason logically, narrate plots of books or films, and handle unexpected situations when traveling.

🧩 Grammar Inventory

  • Past continuous vs past simple; imperfect vs perfective aspect
  • First and second conditional sentences (hypothetical situations)
  • Passive voice for factual and impersonal reporting
  • Relative clauses (defining and non-defining with relative pronouns)
  • Discourse connectors: cause, contrast, consequence, concession

📚 Vocabulary Themes (~2,500–3,200 words)

  • Workplace environments, job applications, modern career paths
  • Media, internet culture, news headlines, entertainment
  • Social trends, environmental issues, travel experiences, dreams & goals
  • Feelings, relationships, character traits, problem-solving exchanges

⚡ Demands & Expectations

  • 2–3 hours of weekly preparation; regular essay drafting (150–200 words)
  • Spontaneous debate without relying on pre-written speaking notes
  • Listening to standard radio broadcasts, podcasts, and video segments

🎯 Can-Do Abilities & Knowledge

  • Connect phrases in a coherent sequence to give reasons and explanations
  • Write straightforward, connected texts on topics of personal or general interest
  • Summarize short articles and respond critically to differing viewpoints
🌲 Level B2: Vantage & Fluency 400 – 600 Guided Hours

Pedagogical Goal: Achieve full operational independence. Understand complex ideas in technical and literary texts; interact with native speakers with a high degree of spontaneity and grammatical accuracy.

🧩 Grammar Inventory

  • Subjunctive / Conjunctive mood (expressing doubt, emotion, necessity)
  • Mixed conditionals and past unfulfilled hypotheses (Third Conditional)
  • Advanced passive, causative structures, and reported speech nuances
  • Participle clauses, gerunds vs infinitives with subtle meaning changes
  • Complex discourse markers for structured argumentation

📚 Vocabulary Themes (~4,500–6,000 words)

  • Global politics, economic trends, ethics, technological disruption
  • Idiomatic expressions, phrasal verbs, collocations, nuances of register
  • Art, cinema, literature analysis, scientific debate
  • Workplace negotiations, persuasive pitch delivery, formal complaints

⚡ Demands & Expectations

  • Critical reading of authentic newspapers, editorials, and non-fiction essays
  • Weekly formal writing tasks (reports, argumentative essays 250–350 words)
  • Ability to self-correct grammatical mistakes dynamically during speech

🎯 Can-Do Abilities & Knowledge

  • Present clear, detailed descriptions on a wide range of subjects related to your field
  • Explain a viewpoint on a topical issue giving the advantages and disadvantages of various options
  • Understand the main ideas of complex text on both concrete and abstract topics
🌳 Level C1: Effective Operational Proficiency 600 – 800 Guided Hours

Pedagogical Goal: Master stylistic nuances, irony, and rhetorical elegance. Use language flexibly and effectively for social, academic, and professional purposes with effortless fluency.

🧩 Grammar Inventory

  • Cleft sentences, inversion, and fronting for dramatic rhetorical emphasis
  • Subtle modal auxiliaries denoting varying degrees of deduction and speculation
  • Nominalization and advanced syntactic synthesis in academic writing
  • Archaic, literary, and vernacular register shifts
  • Ellipsis, substitution, and complex co-reference mechanisms

📚 Vocabulary Themes (~8,000–10,000 words)

  • Philosophical discourse, legal jargon, financial markets, sociology
  • Extensive idiomatic repertoire, cultural metaphors, double entendres
  • Academic prose, scholarly critique, policy analysis
  • Abstract rhetoric, persuasive public oratory, negotiation subtleties

⚡ Demands & Expectations

  • Immersion in uncut native podcasts, political debates, and literary works
  • Writing long-form synthesis papers, academic abstracts, and nuanced reviews
  • Zero hesitation in spontaneous oral debates on controversial topics

🎯 Can-Do Abilities & Knowledge

  • Express ideas fluently and spontaneously without much obvious searching for expressions
  • Formulate ideas and opinions with precision and relate your contribution skillfully to those of other speakers
  • Produce clear, smoothly flowing, well-structured text showing controlled use of organizational patterns
🏛️ Level C2: Mastery & Bilingual Polish 800+ Guided Hours

Pedagogical Goal: Near-native linguistic intuition. Reconstruct arguments and accounts from different spoken and written sources, summarizing them into a coherent presentation with stylistic finesse.

🧩 Grammar Inventory

  • Effortless command of all historical, literary, and regional syntactic variants
  • Micro-nuances of punctuation, prosody, stress, and intonation contouring
  • Total freedom in manipulating sentence length and rhythmic cadence
  • Spontaneous creative linguistic innovation and wordplay

📚 Vocabulary Themes (~15,000+ words)

  • Complete cultural subtext, historical idioms, regional dialects
  • Classical literature, specialized scientific papers, jurisprudence
  • Humor, sarcasm, satire, subtle subtext in literature and film

⚡ Demands & Expectations

  • Deep engagement with original literature, legal briefs, and historical texts
  • Publishable-grade written compositions with flawless stylistic tone
  • Ability to mediate heated cultural disputes with perfect diplomatic tact

🎯 Can-Do Abilities & Knowledge

  • Understand with ease virtually everything heard or read
  • Convey finer shades of meaning precisely even in the most complex situations
  • Advocate persuasively in any professional, diplomatic, or literary arena

How Lessons Work & Pedagogical Method

Every session is structured around our communicative principle where at least 40% of class time is spent in active oral dialogue:

  • Targeted Warm-Up (10 min): Spontaneous recall of previously learned lexical structures through rapid-fire question-and-answer prompts.
  • Grammar in Context (20 min): Inductive grammar exploration using authentic texts, podcasts, or dialogues rather than abstract decontextualized tables.
  • Communicative Practice (20 min): Role-play, scenario resolution, and structured debate requiring active application of new syntax.
  • Active Production & Error Feedback (10 min): Immediate oral recap, real-time correction in shared notes, and customized homework assignments.

Lesson Durations Offered: 60 minutes or 90 minutes.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How do I know which CEFR level I should start at?
Before your first lesson, you will take our free adaptive placement quiz. Additionally, your teacher conducts an initial oral diagnostic evaluation during your trial session to pinpoint your precise baseline across receptive and productive skills.
How long does it take to advance from one CEFR level to the next?
On average, advancing through A1 takes about 80–100 guided hours; A2 takes ~100–120 hours; B1 takes ~180–200 hours; and B2 takes ~200–250 hours. With 2 lessons per week and regular homework, adult students typically advance one sub-level (e.g. B1.1 to B1.2) every 3 to 4 months.
Is there mandatory homework in the General Course?
Yes. Because the General Course is a comprehensive 4-skill curriculum, regular practice between lessons is crucial. You will receive 30–60 minutes of weekly homework comprising reading, writing, and vocabulary drills hosted on COSYplatform. If you want zero homework and only oral dialogue, choose our Spoken Course.
Can I switch between 1-to-1 lessons and group classes?
Yes, our flexible structure lets you transition between individual 1-to-1 coaching and small peer groups when a new cohort opens at your exact CEFR level.
