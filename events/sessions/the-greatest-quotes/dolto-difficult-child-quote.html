Theme

Parenting & Identity

We unpack Françoise Dolto's famous belief about difficult behaviors in childhood. This session explores how expressing disagreement and challenge is necessary for healthy identity development. We will discuss the fine line between loving guidance and restrictive conformity.

☕ The Philosophers' Ledger: Café Debates

"Let us see how many interpretations exist! There is never only one correct answer to any profound human question." — Oscar Wilde

📖 Session Vocabulary

Authenticity
the quality of being genuine, real, or true to oneself.
Living with absolute authenticity brings a profound sense of inner peace.
Conformity
behavior in accordance with socially accepted conventions or standards.
The school rules demanded strict conformity, leaving no room for self-expression.
Autonomy
the right or condition of self-government; personal independence.
The young artist struggled to gain complete personal and financial autonomy.
Suppression
the act of keeping something from happening or being revealed.
The suppression of emotional needs often leads to intense internal conflict.
Rebellion
the action or process of resisting authority, control, or convention.
Her teenage rebellion was a necessary step toward discovering her true self.
Subconscious
the part of the mind which is not fully aware but influences actions.
Our childhood experiences are deeply buried in our subconscious.
Self-discovery
the process of acquiring insight into one's own character and potential.
Her travels in Asia were part of a lifelong journey of self-discovery.
Persona
the aspect of someone's character that is presented to or perceived by others.
He adopted a confident public persona to hide his deep-seated insecurities.
Individuality
the quality or character of a particular person that distinguishes them from others.
The curriculum should celebrate the unique individuality of each student.
Dignity
the state or quality of being worthy of honor or respect.
Every human being has the right to live their life with basic dignity.

🎙️ Discussion Structure

🟠 Warm-up▲
  • Why do you think some children are labeled as 'difficult'?
  • Should parents let children express their difficult feelings freely?
🔵 Round 1 — Analysis▲
How does the pursuit of Authenticity influence our understanding of childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery?
★ When was the last time you had to cultivate Authenticity in your journey with childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery?
Is it possible to find true balance in childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery when Conformity is either completely absent or excessive?
★ How comfortable are you with allowing Conformity to guide your daily decisions regarding childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery?
Why do we often struggle with Autonomy even when we seek to embrace childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery?
★ Have you ever felt a deep sense of conflict regarding Autonomy in a relationship centered on childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery?
How does Suppression reshape our emotional landscape when we face challenges in childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery?
★ Can you share a moment from your childhood where Suppression felt completely natural in relation to childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery?
Can Rebellion act as a silent shield against social conformity and support childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery?
★ How do you personally nurture Rebellion when you feel exhausted by childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery?
In what ways does Subconscious challenge the rigid boundaries of logical thinking inside childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery?
★ Have you ever had to choose between social conformity and personal Subconscious for the sake of childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery?
How do our childhood experiences shape our relationship with Self-discovery when we think about childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery?
★ Who in your life best embodies the essence of Self-discovery in the context of childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery?
How does embracing Persona change the way we handle interpersonal conflict within childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery?
★ In what area of your life do you feel Persona is most desperately needed right now to support childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery?
Is Individuality a quality of quiet solitude, or does it thrive in communities dedicated to childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery?
★ How has your personal understanding of Individuality in relation to childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery shifted over the last five years?
What is the most profound lesson that Dignity can teach us about our own nature and childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery?
★ If you had to describe your personal experience with Dignity under the lens of childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery in one word, what would it be?
🟣 Let's Speak Together▲

A comparative reflection on our habits and self-perceptions.

🟢 Round 2 — Philosophical Application▲
In a future world built around childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery, our collective approach to Authenticity will become a core social rule rather than a personal choice. — Do you agree or disagree?
★ How would you adapt if Authenticity was governed by this new social rule?
As digital technology advances, applying the philosophy of childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery will make Conformity completely free from corporate control. — Do you agree or disagree?
★ Would you trust a digital system that uses this philosophy to manage your Conformity?
Future educational systems that prioritize the wisdom of childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery will value the cultivation of Autonomy over traditional academic success. — Do you agree or disagree?
★ If you could design a futuristic school around this idea, how would you teach the value of Autonomy?
If artificial intelligence is programmed to follow the philosophy of childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery, it will exhibit a superior capacity for Suppression compared to humans. — Do you agree or disagree?
★ Do you fear a future where machines understand Suppression better than humans do?
The future survival of human civilization will depend on our ability to preserve Rebellion by practicing the philosophy of childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery. — Do you agree or disagree?
★ What role do you hope Rebellion and this philosophy will play in your own personal future?
In the coming decades, if we ignore the lessons of childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery, true Subconscious will become a highly commercialized luxury. — Do you agree or disagree?
★ Would you be willing to pay to protect your personal experience of Subconscious?
Global digital connectivity will make the philosophy of childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery almost impossible to maintain, destroying our capacity for Self-discovery. — Do you agree or disagree?
★ How do you personally protect your Self-discovery from the constant noise of the digital world?
Future communities built on the foundation of childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery will value Persona far more than physical wealth or social status. — Do you agree or disagree?
★ Would you choose to live in a community that completely guarantees Persona at the cost of your convenience?
If future generations completely reject the wisdom of childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery, they will experience a profound crisis of Individuality. — Do you agree or disagree?
★ What message about Individuality and this philosophy would you send to someone living 100 years from now?
A future world governed entirely by logic and efficiency will have no space left for the quiet mystery of Dignity and childhood rebellion and authentic self-discovery. — Do you agree or disagree?
★ If a machine could perfectly replicate your experience of Dignity under this philosophy, would you still value it?
✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
He have went to Paris → He has gone to Paris (Present perfect third-person singular 'has' + past participle 'gone'.)
We discussed about the problem → We discussed the problem / had a discussion about the problem ('Discuss' is transitive and takes no preposition, or use 'have a discussion about'.)
I am agree with you → I agree with you (Use 'agree' directly as a verb rather than using it with the auxiliary 'am'.)
