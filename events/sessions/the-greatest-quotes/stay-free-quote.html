Theme

Identity & Freedom

This session explores the profound idea that true freedom begins with the choice to be ourselves despite external pressures. We will delve into the concepts of authenticity and social conformity, examining why it is often difficult to remain independent in a world of expectations. Participants will discuss the balance between societal roles and personal identity in their quest to "stay free".

📖 Session Vocabulary

Choice
the act of selecting or making a decision when faced with possibilities.
We might not choose where we are born, but we always have a choice to stay true to our values.
Freedom
the power or right to act, speak, or think as one wants without hindrance or control.
To stay free means to protect your individual mind from social expectations.
Identity
the unique qualities, beliefs, and characteristics that make a person who they are.
The constant pressure to conform can completely erase a person's individual identity.
Pressure
strong social influence or force that encourages someone to behave in a certain way.
Many people feel intense pressure to follow traditional life paths.
Conform
to adjust one's behavior or thinking to match the accepted standards of a group.
When you choose to conform, you sacrifice a piece of your unique self-expression.
Genuine
real, authentic, and true to one's own thoughts, feelings, and character.
Living a genuine life brings a profound sense of inner peace and freedom.
Decision
a conclusion or resolution reached after consideration and choosing among options.
Every decision we make either moves us closer to our true self or further away.
Independent
capable of thinking, deciding, and acting on one's own without relying on social approval.
An independent mind is highly valuable in a world that rewards quiet conformity.
Values
the important principles or beliefs that guide a person's behavior and choices.
If you do not define your own values, others will happily define them for you.
Expectations
the beliefs or demands of others regarding how we should behave or live.
It is incredibly freeing to realize that you needn't live your life to meet others' expectations.

⚡ Grammar Practice — Subjunctive Mood for Deep Reflection▲

⚡ Grammar Explanation (Intermediate (B1))

Use the Subjunctive Mood (I wish I were, it is essential that he be, if only she had) to express hypothetical wishes, urgent recommendations, or regrets about life.

📝 Task 1 — Part A: Rule Formulation:

Complete the rule: After verbs of demanding, recommending or wishing, the subjunctive uses the _____ of the verb (e.g. 'be', 'understand'). For hypothetical regrets about the past, use past modal + _____ + past participle.

📝 Task 1 — Part B: Structured Practice:
  1. It is essential that we _____ true to ourselves rather than convenient.
  2. I wish our society _____ more focused on spiritual growth.
  3. If only he _____ his vulnerability before it was too late.
  4. They recommended that she _____ the nature of her soul in silence.
  5. We demanded that the organization _____ individual human dignity.

🟣 Let's Speak Together — The Freedom vs. Conformity Spectrum 🧘▲

Discuss with your partner: In which scenario is it most difficult to stay free and remain genuine? Rank them:

🏢 Career & Corporate Life
When you must conform to strict corporate values and expectations to protect your job stability.
🏡 Family Traditions & Rules
When your parents' heavy expectations clash directly with your independent life choices.
🌐 Online Networks & Media
When peer pressure and digital likes push you to perform a fake persona rather than stay genuine.
👥 Close Friends & Community
When you must hide your authentic values to stay accepted in your core social circle.

Use the subjunctive mood (I wish, it is essential that) to justify your views!

🏁 Closing Circle: Complete the Sentence▲

Finish the session by going in a circle. Each student must complete the following sentence with exactly one adjective or short phrase using the vocabulary learned:

"Staying free is..."

Examples: a personal choice, genuine living, resisting pressure, choosing our identity, harder than conformity.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
He have went to Paris → He has gone to Paris (Present perfect third-person singular 'has' + past participle 'gone'.)
We discussed about the problem → We discussed the problem / had a discussion about the problem ('Discuss' is transitive and takes no preposition, or use 'have a discussion about'.)
I am agree with you → I agree with you (Use 'agree' directly as a verb rather than using it with the auxiliary 'am'.)
