❤️ Core Concepts & Feelings

right
morally good, justified, or acceptable; correct.
She did the right thing by telling the truth to her friend.
Opposite: wrong
Wilderness
an uncultivated, uninhabited, and inhospitable region.
They went hiking in the beautiful mountain wilderness.
Opposite: Civilization
Warm
having or showing a moderate degree of heat.
A warm bowl of soup is perfect for a cold day.
Opposite: Cold
love
an intense feeling of deep affection.
Their shared love for classical music brought them together.
everything
all things, or all things of a particular class.
They lost everything in the fire but were grateful to be safe.

🗣️ Life, Actions & Connection

day
a period of twenty-four hours, or the time of light.
It was a beautiful day to spend outdoors in the countryside.
better
of a more excellent or effective type or quality.
She was feeling much better after taking a short, peaceful nap.
naturopath
a practitioner of alternative medicine.
The naturopath suggested using herbal teas to relieve the stress.
nightlife
social activities or entertainment available at night in a town or city.
The vibrant city is famous for its diverse and active nightlife.

✨ Stage 5: Language Focus▲

Language Focus: Comparative Continuous Upgrade (*getting better*)

In the song Its Getting Better, the repetitive lyric chunk "It's getting better all the time" highlights the use of Comparative Continuous Upgrade (*getting better*). This structure supports: Tracking positive recoveries and hope.

Let's Practise — Three Examples:
  • Example 1: Practice using this structure actively during the discussion rounds.
  • Example 2: Notice how this pattern is repeated to build rhythmic familiarity.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
There is a big improvement of my English → There is a big improvement in my English (Use 'in' after 'improvement' to express domain.)
It was a great relief of hearing the news → It was a great relief to hear the news (Use 'relief' + infinitive 'to hear'.)
She is in the choir since three years → She has been in the choir for three years (Use present perfect + 'for' to show duration of state.)
