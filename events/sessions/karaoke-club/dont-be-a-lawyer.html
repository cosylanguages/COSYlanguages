📚 Stage 2: Vocabulary▲

❤️ Core Concepts & Feelings

Lucrative
producing a great deal of profit; profitable.
He decided to leave his low-paying job to pursue a more lucrative career in tech.
Opposite: Unprofitable
Prestigious
inspiring respect and admiration; having high status.
Glendale University is not exactly a prestigious Ivy League school.
Opposite: Obscure
Vocation
a strong feeling of suitability for a particular career or occupation; a calling.
She always felt that teaching was her true vocation, not just a way to pay the bills.
Opposite: Chore
Hesitation
the action of pausing or hesitating before saying or doing something.
She accepted the job offer without a moment's hesitation.
Opposite: Alacrity
Ruin
to damage or destroy something completely.
Working eighty hours a week is the quickest way to ruin your mental health.
Opposite: Restore

🗣️ Life, Actions & Connection

Inherently
in a permanent, essential, or characteristic way.
Many people believe that corporate law is inherently stressful and unhappy.
Opposite: Superficially
Debt
something, typically money, that is owed or due.
Going to law school left him with a mountain of student debt.
Opposite: Asset
Regret
feel sad, repentant, or disappointed over.
He expressed deep regret for his past mistakes.
Opposite: Satisfaction
Expertise
expert skill or knowledge in a particular field.
His primary expertise lies in tax law and small business consulting.
Opposite: Incompetence
Avoid
to keep away from or stop oneself from doing something.
If you want to be happy, you should avoid toxic work environments.
Opposite: Confront

✨ Stage 5: Language Focus▲

Imperative Verbs & Modals of Strong Advice ('should / must / ought to')

The song uses direct imperatives (e.g., 'Don't be a lawyer!') to express powerful warnings. Combined with modals of strong advice like 'should', 'must', and 'ought to', we can construct compelling, persuasive arguments about life, work, and following one's passion.

Let's Practise — Three Examples:
  • Don't study law unless you are prepared to spend eighty hours a week workin' on pharmaceutical mergers.
  • You should avoid choosing a lucrative career if it inherently ruins your peace of mind.
  • We must listen to our true vocation instead of rushing into decisions with too much hesitation.

There is a big improvement of my English → There is a big improvement in my English (Use 'in' after 'improvement' to express domain.)
It was a great relief of hearing the news → It was a great relief to hear the news (Use 'relief' + infinitive 'to hear'.)
She is in the choir since three years → She has been in the choir for three years (Use present perfect + 'for' to show duration of state.)
