Primary Hypothesis

Experimental Findings

Induced signaling cascades initiate localized cellular de-differentiation.

Linguistic Focus

Hypothetical 'wishes' and 'if only' constructs.

This session covers recent studies suggesting that human tissues possess hidden regenerative capabilities similar to some lower vertebrates. We will discuss the biological mechanisms of wound healing and the potential of regenerative medicine to restore damaged organs. We will also touch upon the future ethical implications and possibilities of longevity sciences.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Regeneration
the process of growing back lost body parts or tissues.
Lizards are famous for their rapid tail regeneration.
Somatic
relating to the physical body rather than the mind.
Humans have limited somatic healing capabilities.
Heal
to become healthy or sound again after an injury.
Minor cuts on your skin will heal within a few days.
Dormant
inactive or sleeping, but capable of becoming active.
Many desert plants remain dormant during the dry summer.
Cell
the smallest structural and functional unit of an organism.
Our body's stem cells help repair damaged tissue.
Active
engaging or ready to engage in physically energetic pursuits.
The dormant genes became active after the chemical treatment.
Trigger
to cause an event or situation to happen or exist.
Severe injuries trigger a complex healing response.
Somatic
relating to bodily tissues and systems.
The medical team observed profound somatic recovery.
Restore
to return something to its original or former condition.
The therapy helped restore full movement to his leg.
Ability
the physical or mental power to do something.
Humans lack the ability to grow back entire limbs.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
The lizards can to regrow tail→Lizards can regrow their tails(Can + infinitive without 'to', plural noun)
He was operated last week→He had an operation last week(Passive structure vs having an operation)
I am agree with scientifics→I agree with the scientists(Agree is a verb, noun 'scientists')
