National Someone Day is a beautiful opportunity to thank that special 'someone' who has made a difference in your life. Whether it is a helpful neighbor, a dedicated teacher, or a supportive coworker, this session is all about recognizing unsung kindness. We will discuss the positive impact of showing gratitude and how simple gestures can strengthen our daily connections. Join us for a lively B2 debate.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Gratitude
the feeling of being thankful and showing appreciation.
Cultivating deep gratitude in our daily lives has been shown to improve mental well-being.
Appreciation
the recognition of the good qualities of someone or something.
We must show real appreciation for the people who work silently to keep our communities clean.
Unsung
not praised, celebrated, or praised publicly despite doing valuable work.
Cleaners, delivery riders, and social workers are the unsung heroes of our modern cities.
Acknowledge
to accept or admit the truth, existence, or value of something.
It is vital to acknowledge the contributions of every single team member before finishing a project.
Kindness
the quality of being friendly, generous, and considerate.
A small act of kindness can start a positive chain reaction throughout the workplace.
Supportive
providing encouragement, help, or emotional assistance to someone.
Working in a highly supportive environment makes it much easier to handle stressful deadlines.
Apathy ≠ Empathy
a lack of enthusiasm, interest, or concern ≠ the ability to understand and share the feelings of another.
We must actively fight social apathy by teaching empathy and community values in schools.
Reciprocate
to respond to a gesture or action by making a corresponding one.
When someone does you a favor, it is natural to want to reciprocate their kindness in the future.
Altruistic
showing a disinterested and selfless concern for the well-being of others.
His decision to donate all his prize money to the local children's hospital was purely altruistic.
Nurture
to care for and encourage the growth or development of someone or something.
We need to nurture our close relationships by spending quality time together on a regular basis.

🎙️ Discussion Structure

🟠 Warm-up▲
  • Who is a special 'someone' that helped you this month?
  • Do you find it easy to say 'thank you' in public?
🔵 Round 1 — General Discussion▲
Quiz / Discussion
How do you express your sincere gratitude to a helpful coworker?
★ When was the last time you felt true gratitude for a friend's help?
Why is showing public appreciation so important in a modern company?
★ Do you feel appreciated by your colleagues or managers?
Why do we often forget to celebrate the unsung heroes in our local communities?
★ Who is an unsung hero in your personal life that you wish you thanked more?
Is it difficult to acknowledge your own mistakes when working in a group?
★ Have you ever had to acknowledge a major mistake to a supportive boss?
What small act of kindness from a stranger has stuck with you over the years?
★ What kind gesture did you do for someone recently?
What are the characteristics of an exceptionally supportive friend?
★ Who is the most supportive person in your life right now?
How can society combat growing levels of apathy toward marginalized groups?
★ Have you ever felt a sense of apathy in your local community regarding social issues?
Is it necessary to immediately reciprocate a favor, or can it feel transactional?
★ How do you feel when someone does you a favor and expects you to reciprocate immediately?
Are humans truly capable of purely altruistic actions, or is there always a selfish motive?
★ Have you ever done something completely altruistic with absolutely zero expectation of return?
What is the best way to nurture a long-distance friendship over several years?
★ What long-distance relationship or friendship have you worked hard to nurture?
🟣 Let's Speak Together▲

Interactive task: Design a perfect 'Thank You' card for a supportive coworker who helped you with a very hard task on a tight deadline.

🟢 Round 2 — Agree or Disagree: The Future▲
Debate
In the future, technology will make it impossible to nurture genuine face-to-face friendships.
★ Do you think technology helps or hurts your personal effort to nurture connections?
We will rely entirely on AI models to acknowledge and validate our feelings instead of human counselors.
★ Would you ask an AI to help you acknowledge and process a difficult emotion?
Future societies will conquer social apathy by implementing mandatory volunteering hours for all citizens.
★ Do you think compulsory community service is an effective way to cure social apathy?
We will build a reputation-based economy where we can only access services if we regularly reciprocate favors.
★ Would you feel comfortable in an economy where you must constantly reciprocate favors to maintain your social score?
Public appreciation will become a digital currency, where we tip municipal workers with 'like' tokens.
★ Do you believe tipping digital tokens of appreciation is better than traditional tipping?
We will use biometric sensors to immediately broadcast our gratitude without having to say a single word.
★ Would you use a sensor to express gratitude, or is a face-to-face speech irreplaceable?
Purely altruistic gestures will disappear because social media forces everyone to document their good deeds.
★ Does filming an altruistic act for TikTok ruin the sincerity of the gesture?
Our small daily acts of kindness will be tracked by corporate algorithms to give us discount vouchers.
★ Would you want your random acts of kindness to be tracked and rewarded with discounts?
Unsung workers will form unions to demand automated robots replace them in dangerous tasks.
★ Would you prefer to see dangerous jobs handled by robots rather than unsung human workers?
A highly supportive culture where criticism is forbidden will lead to weaker and less innovative ideas.
★ Do you think constant supportive feedback can sometimes hold back real progress?
✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
I am very grateful of my teacher. → I am very grateful to my teacher / grateful for my teacher's help. (Preposition check: 'grateful' is followed by 'to' for people, and 'for' for actions/things.)
We discussed about our someone special. → We discussed our special someone. (Preposition error: the verb 'discuss' is transitive and does not take 'about'.)
It is very important to nurturing friendships. → It is very important to nurture friendships. (Infinitive structure: 'to' is followed by the base form of the verb 'nurture'.)
