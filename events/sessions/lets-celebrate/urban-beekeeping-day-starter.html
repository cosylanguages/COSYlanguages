Welcome to Urban Beekeeping Day! This is a simple session for beginners. We talk about small bees in the big city. We learn simple words about nature, flowers, and sweet honey. Let's speak English together!

📖 Session Vocabulary

Bee
a small yellow and black insect that makes honey.
I see a small bee on a flower.
Honey
a very sweet yellow food made by bees.
I eat honey with my bread.
Garden
a green place next to a house with plants and flowers.
My house has a small garden with green grass.
Flower
the beautiful and colored part of a plant.
This red flower smells very nice.
Tree
a very tall green plant in nature.
There is a big green tree in front of my window.
Help
to make something easy or good for someone.
Please help me plant these flowers.
Sweet
having a pleasant taste of sugar.
This hot tea is very sweet because it has sugar.
City
a very big town with many houses and streets.
I live in a big and noisy city.
House
a building where people live.
My parents live in a small white house.
Insect
a very small animal with six legs.
A bee is a useful insect.

⚡ Grammar Practice — Simple Present & Singular/Plural Nouns

⚡ Grammar Explanation (Starter / Beginner (A1))

Use Simple Present for general facts, matching the singular or plural forms of the nouns.

📝 Task 1 — Part A: Rule Formulation:

Complete the rule: Plural nouns usually end with _____. When the subject is singular (e.g., 'a bee'), the verb takes _____. When the subject is plural (e.g., 'bees'), we use the _____ verb.
(Key Options: -s, -s/-es, base)

📝 Task 1 — Part B: Structured Practice:
  • A beekeeper _____ (help) the bees in the garden. (Answer: helps)
  • Many beautiful _____ (flower) grow near my house. (Answer: flowers)
  • Bees _____ (make) sweet honey. (Answer: make)
  • This small insect _____ (fly) very fast. (Answer: flies)
  • Two green _____ (tree) stand in front of my window. (Answer: trees)
🗣️ Task 2 — Interactive Speaking:

Look around your room. Name 3 singular objects (e.g., 'a chair') and 3 plural objects (e.g., 'books') and describe what they are or do.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
The bee have beautiful wings. → The bee has beautiful wings. (Subject-verb agreement: 'The bee' is singular, use 'has'.)
I saw a bee on yesterday. → I saw a bee yesterday. (Time adverb: do not use the preposition 'on' before 'yesterday'.)
He is afraid to bees. → He is afraid of bees. (Preposition check: the adjective 'afraid' is followed by the preposition 'of'.)
