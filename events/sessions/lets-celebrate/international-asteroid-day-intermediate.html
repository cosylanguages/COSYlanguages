Theme

Cosmic Rocks and Planet Safety

International Asteroid Day raises awareness about the risks of space rocks hitting Earth and how we can protect our home. This B1 session explores the history of large collisions, how astronomers find objects in space, and what we can do to stay safe. Join us for a fascinating conversation about our solar system.

🔞
Sensitive Topic Note: This session focuses on sensitive or challenging personal and philosophical themes. Discussion is intended to be handled with extreme respect, empathy, and academic focus.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Asteroid
a small, rocky object that travels around the Sun.
We can use strong telescopes to watch a distant asteroid move across the sky.
Impact
the act of one object hitting another with force.
A massive asteroid impact would create a dust cloud that could block sunlight globally.
Threat
a possibility of trouble, danger, or harm.
A rogue space rock remains a constant existential threat to our modern, hyper-dependent digital networks.
Detect
to notice or discover something that is difficult to see.
Smoke alarms help us detect small fires before they become dangerous.
Orbit
the curved path of an object around a star, planet, or moon.
The Moon takes about twenty-seven days to complete its orbit around Earth.
Space
the empty area outside Earth's atmosphere where stars and planets are located.
Many children dream of flying in a rocket ship into deep space.
Protect
to keep someone or something safe from injury, damage, or loss.
You should wear a strong helmet to protect your head while riding a bicycle.
Predict
to say that an event will happen in the future.
It is very difficult to predict the exact score of a football match.
Scientist
a person who studies or has expert knowledge of natural sciences.
The young scientist spent years researching new green energy sources.
Safe ≠ Dangerous
not in danger or likely to be harmed ≠ likely to cause harm or injury.
It is safe to cross the street here, but walking on the highway is dangerous.
