International Asteroid Day raises awareness about the risks of space rocks hitting Earth and how we can protect our home. This B1 session explores the history of large collisions, how astronomers find objects in space, and what we can do to stay safe. Join us for a fascinating conversation about our solar system.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Asteroid
a small, rocky object that travels around the Sun.
We can use strong telescopes to watch a distant asteroid move across the sky.
Impact
the act of one object hitting another with force.
The heavy impact of the soccer ball broke the window.
Threat
a possibility of trouble, danger, or harm.
Dark clouds in the afternoon are a clear threat of rain.
Detect
to notice or discover something that is difficult to see.
Smoke alarms help us detect small fires before they become dangerous.
Orbit
the curved path of an object around a star, planet, or moon.
The Moon takes about twenty-seven days to complete its orbit around Earth.
Space
the empty area outside Earth's atmosphere where stars and planets are located.
Many children dream of flying in a rocket ship into deep space.
Protect
to keep someone or something safe from injury, damage, or loss.
You should wear a strong helmet to protect your head while riding a bicycle.
Predict
to say that an event will happen in the future.
It is very difficult to predict the exact score of a football match.
Scientist
a person who studies or has expert knowledge of natural sciences.
The young scientist spent years researching new green energy sources.
Safe ≠ Dangerous
not in danger or likely to be harmed ≠ likely to cause harm or injury.
It is safe to cross the street here, but walking on the highway is dangerous.

🎙️ Discussion Structure

🟠 Warm-up▲
  • What is the first thing that comes to your mind when you think of "outer space"?
  • Would you ever want to travel to space if you had the chance?
🔵 Round 1 — General Discussion▲
Quiz / Discussion
What is the scientific difference between a shooting star and a large asteroid?
★ Have you ever seen a shooting star in the night sky?
How would a major space rock impact affect the climate of our planet?
★ Have you ever experienced a sudden weather change that disrupted your plans?
Why do some people worry about space objects while others see zero threat?
★ Do you worry about global issues, or do you focus on daily problems?
How do telescopes detect small objects that are extremely far away?
★ Do you enjoy using binoculars or telescopes to look at nature?
Why do planets and asteroids stay in a stable orbit around the Sun?
★ Do you find it easy to keep a stable routine in your own life?
Why is exploring deep space so exciting for young students?
★ What was your favorite science subject when you were in school?
What can we do to protect our local forests and rivers from pollution?
★ Do you recycle or take other actions to protect the environment?
Is it really possible to predict the future of our planet's climate?
★ Do you like reading predictions about new technologies?
What kind of work does a space scientist do every day?
★ Would you enjoy working in a laboratory or doing research?
What makes a neighborhood feel completely safe and friendly?
★ Do you feel safe walking around your city at night?
🟣 Let's Speak Together▲

Interactive task: Design a simple poster warning people about the dangers of space debris and explaining how to stay safe.

🟢 Round 2 — Agree or Disagree: The Future▲
Debate
In the future, a private company will own every large asteroid in our solar system.
★ Would you support companies mining resources in outer space?
We will build a giant laser shield in space to destroy any threat before it reaches Earth.
★ Do you think space technology is a good use of government money?
Every country will have its own planetary defense scientist team to monitor the skies.
★ Do you think your country should hire more scientists?
A major threat from a space rock will force all nations to stop fighting and work together.
★ Do you agree that a common danger makes people cooperate?
Computers will become so smart they will predict exact weather patterns ten years in advance.
★ Would you want to know the weather ten years from now?
We will use special rockets to change the orbit of any dangerous rock heading our way.
★ Do you believe science can solve almost any physical problem?
Future cities will be built underground to keep everyone completely safe from space impacts.
★ Would you feel comfortable living in an underground city?
We will use smart satellites to immediately detect when a volcano is going to erupt.
★ Do you think technology can make natural disasters less dangerous?
An asteroid impact will destroy all human libraries, leaving no record of our history.
★ Do you keep backup copies of your important photos and documents?
We will eventually have to leave Earth to protect our species from extinction.
★ Would you move to Mars if Earth became too dangerous?
✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
An asteroid impact in Earth is dangerous. → An asteroid impact on Earth is dangerous. (Preposition check: use 'on' with 'impact' when referring to the object hit.)
Scientists can to predict where it goes. → Scientists can predict where it goes. (Modal verb error: 'can' is followed by the bare infinitive 'predict' without 'to'.)
Space is very danger. → Space is very dangerous. (Word class: 'danger' is a noun; use the adjective 'dangerous' to describe a place.)
