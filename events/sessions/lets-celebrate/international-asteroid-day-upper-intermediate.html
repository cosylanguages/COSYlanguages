International Asteroid Day, observed on the anniversary of the 1908 Tunguska event, raises awareness about the risks of asteroid impacts and the science of planetary defense. This B2 session explores the history of significant impacts and the technological breakthroughs being made to detect and divert near-Earth objects. We will discuss the balance between rare cosmic threats and immediate planetary challenges. Join us for an advanced B2 conversation.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Asteroid
a small rocky object that travels around the Sun.
Astronomers are tracking a massive asteroid that will pass relatively close to Earth next decade.
Impact
a collision or powerful effect between two large bodies.
The physical impact of a medium-sized space rock could wipe out an entire metropolitan area.
Threat
a state of danger or a possibility of severe trouble or harm.
A solar storm represents a minor threat to our electrical grid systems.
Detect
to discover, trace, or identify the presence or existence of something.
New sensory networks can detect dark cosmic debris long before it enters our orbit.
Orbit
the regular, curved path of a celestial object or spacecraft around a star or planet.
We can successfully map the gravitational orbit of near-Earth objects to calculate collisions.
Awareness
conscious knowledge, understanding, or perception of a situation or fact.
Raising public awareness about environmental hazards is critical for long-term safety.
Predict
to estimate or state that a specific future event will occur.
Mathematicians can predict planetary motions with outstanding accuracy.
Survival ≠ Extinction
the state or fact of continuing to live or exist ≠ the complete wiping out of a species.
While human survival is currently stable, many fragile animal species face immediate extinction.
Planetary defense
active international programs designed to protect Earth from dangerous celestial objects.
Several space agencies are collaborating on planetary defense tests to alter space rock trajectories.
Breakthrough
a sudden, dramatic, and important discovery or development.
The discovery of a new rocket fuel was a major breakthrough for deep space travel.

🎙️ Discussion Structure

🟠 Warm-up▲
  • What is the first thing that comes to your mind when you think of "outer space"?
  • Would you ever want to travel to space if you had the chance?
🔵 Round 1 — General Discussion▲
Quiz / Discussion
What is the scientific difference between a harmless meteor shower and a potentially hazardous asteroid approaching our atmosphere?
★ Have you ever worried about a natural or space-related danger?
Why should modern nations invest in global planetary defense systems when we face so many pressing social crises here on Earth?
★ If you had to choose, what global problem would you invest in first?
How do astronomers use advanced telescope networks to detect dark, non-reflective space rocks before they get too close?
★ Do you usually prefer to detect problems early in your own life, or deal with them when they appear?
What kind of technological breakthrough is needed to successfully divert or destroy a massive object heading toward us?
★ Which recent scientific breakthrough has impressed you the most?
How does plotting the precise gravitational orbit of near-Earth objects help scientists determine the exact probability of a collision?
★ Have you ever learned something that completely changed how you understood a situation?
Why is public awareness about long-term space hazards so much lower than our concern for immediate environmental crises?
★ What topic do you think deserves much more public attention?
What would be the catastrophic environmental and climatic impact if a medium-sized space rock landed in the middle of an ocean?
★ Which historical event do you think changed the world the most?
How do science fiction movies about a space-related threat shape the public's understanding of real astronomical science?
★ Have you ever watched a disaster movie that made you think differently about real life?
How far in advance can astronomers realistically predict a collision, and how much time would humanity have to prepare?
★ Are you someone who likes predicting the future, or do you prefer surprises?
Why is global solidarity and cooperation essential for the survival of our species when facing an existential threat from space?
★ Which countries do you think cooperate especially well in science today?

🟢 Round 2 — Agree or Disagree: The Future▲
Debate
Governments will eventually hide data about an approaching asteroid to prevent global panic and maintain social order.
★ Would you prefer to know the truth about an approaching asteroid even if nothing could be done to stop it?
In the future, asteroid mining corporations will intentionally divert space rocks, risking a collision for resource collection, which represents a massive threat to humanity.
★ Do you think space-based corporate greed is a realistic threat to our safety?
Advanced AI systems will eventually make all planetary defense decisions, completely removing human error from the equation.
★ Would you trust an automated AI system to make split-second planetary defense decisions?
As technology improves, some nations might fabricate a fake emergency claim to predict nonexistent risks and justify space weaponization.
★ How skeptical are you when experts predict an invisible, external danger?
Public 'asteroid awareness fatigue' will eventually set in, leading people to ignore real emergency warnings as scientific alarmism.
★ Do you ever feel we need more awareness campaigns, or are there too many news warnings?
The technology to alter a space rock's orbit will create dangerous geopolitical conflicts over who decides where the object is redirected.
★ If we had to alter an object's orbit, how should humanity decide who takes the risk of redirection?
Humanity's long-term survival will eventually depend on colonizing other planets rather than defending Earth from space hazards.
★ Would you join a space colony if it meant saving human survival?
Private space tourism companies will eventually offer wealthy tourists front-row seats to detect passing rocks in real-time.
★ Would you pay for a voyage to detect and view spectacular cosmic events up close?
In the distant future, a scientific breakthrough in energy shields will unite all human nations into a borderless global government.
★ Why does it seem to take an existential breakthrough or external danger to get humans to cooperate?
A major asteroid impact will eventually be seen as a natural 'reset' button for Earth's ecosystem that humans should not interfere with.
★ Do you believe humanity has a moral duty to prevent a catastrophic impact, or are we part of nature's cycle?
✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
An asteroid impact in Earth is dangerous. → An asteroid impact on Earth is dangerous. (Preposition check: use 'on' with 'impact' when referring to the object hit.)
Scientists can to predict where it goes. → Scientists can predict where it goes. (Modal verb error: 'can' is followed by the bare infinitive 'predict' without 'to'.)
Space is very danger. → Space is very dangerous. (Word class: 'danger' is a noun; use the adjective 'dangerous' to describe a place.)
