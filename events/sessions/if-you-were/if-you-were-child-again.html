Used to & Wishes (Wish / If only)

This session invites students to travel back in time, stepping into the unburdened world of childhood with the benefit of adult knowledge. We discuss creative play, simple pleasures, nostalgically remembered spaces, and spontaneous exploration. Students explore how reclaiming early wonder can heal adult stress and redesign our priorities.

Reality 🌎

Life is filled with strict schedules, financial pressures, adult responsibilities, and constant future anxieties.

The Mirror ⚖️

Life is an open playground. The world is filled with endless imagination, spontaneous games, and naive curiosity.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Carefree
Free from anxiety or responsibility.
When you were seven, your summer days felt completely carefree, with no schedules or deadlines.
Naive
Showing a lack of experience, wisdom, or judgment; innocent and trusting.
Looking back, our naive childhood beliefs about the world were exceptionally sweet and simple.
Nostalgic
Feeling or expressing a sentimental longing for the past.
We often get nostalgic when we smell a childhood dish or see an old playground.
Imagination
The faculty of forming new ideas, or images of external objects not present to the senses.
With a little imagination, a simple cardboard box could become a high-tech spaceship.
Curiosity
A strong desire to know or learn something.
Our native curiosity drove us to catch bugs, climb trees, and ask endless questions.
Spontaneous
Performed or occurring as a result of a sudden impulse, without premeditation.
Children are incredibly spontaneous, starting a new game instantly without hours of planning.
Unburdened
Free from worry, stress, or heavy emotional loads.
If you were unburdened by financial worries, how would you spend your morning play?
Wander
To walk or move in a leisurely, casual, or aimless way.
We used to wander around the neighborhood on our bicycles until the streetlights came on.
Simple
Easily understood or done; presenting no difficulty; plain.
Childhood pleasures were so simple, like eating ice cream on a hot day.
Protect
To keep safe from harm or injury.
It is essential that adults protect children’s innocence, allowing them to remain naive for a while.

🎙️ Discussion Structure

🟠 Warm-up: The Nostalgic Memory▲

Complete the sentence: "If I could play one childhood game again right now, it would be..."

  • What was the name of your best friend when you were eight years old?
  • What is the earliest childhood smell or taste you can clearly remember?
🔵 Round 1 — Inside the Shoes (Radical Empathy)▲
First-Person Roleplay
If you could spend one full day with your childhood imagination but keep your adult knowledge, what would you spend it doing?
★ What was your absolute favorite imaginary world or story when you were a child?
If you were suddenly unburdened by bills and jobs, how would you fill your afternoon without technology?
★ Do you find it difficult to disconnect from your phone during your free weekends?
Do you sometimes feel nostalgic for a time when your only task was to play and wander in the yard?
★ What physical place from your childhood do you miss the most today?
How would you react if you woke up tomorrow and realized you had to live through school rules again, feeling young and naive?
★ Were you an obedient student who followed every school rule, or did you like to break them?
How would you use your native curiosity to explore a new city, as though you had never seen a map?
★ When visiting a new town, do you prefer following a guidebook or getting lost on purpose?
Do you believe it is essential that parents protect their children from knowing about global problems?
★ How old were you when you first started paying attention to global news and politics?
If you had to choose a completely spontaneous activity right now, what would you do?
★ What is the most spontaneous trip or decision you have ever made in your life?
Why are children able to find absolute joy in such simple things like mud or colorful stones?
★ What is one simple, inexpensive activity that always improves your mood today?
If you were a carefree child again, what adult worry would you laugh at the most?
★ What adult responsibility did you think was extremely exciting when you were seven?
If you could write a letter to your childhood self, what would you tell them about their future?
★ Are you currently living the life that your seven-year-old self dreamed of?
🟣 Let's Speak Together: The Play Archetype▲

Which childhood setting would you choose to explore today?

🎒
The Safe Playground

Comfortable boundaries, toys, supervised games, and total security under adult protection.

🚲
The Unsupervised Adventure

Riding bikes to the local river, climbing tall trees, exploring abandoned buildings, and taking small risks.

🟢 Round 2 — The Echo Chamber (Speculative Projections)▲
Global Ripples
1. If governments legally mandated a one-month "childhood sabbatical" for all adults annually, global burnout rates would drop to zero.
2. In the future, children will grow up in fully digital virtual realities, making physical sandboxes and slides obsolete.
3. If toys were banned, children’s native imagination and spontaneous play would increase dramatically.
4. In a world with zero adult supervision, children would build a highly efficient and peaceful society on their own.
5. Future education will completely reject classrooms, allowing children to wander and learn from nature until age twelve.
6. If we protected child naive beliefs forever, global innovation and scientific progress would stop.
7. One day, advanced tech will let adults temporarily store their mature memories, experiencing a weekend with total carefree childhood perception.
8. If simple lifestyles were celebrated, high-stress corporate consumerism would naturally disappear.
9. In the future, adults will be required to consult children before making major ecological and urban planning decisions.
10. If childhood memories were completely erasable, people would live much happier adult lives without nostalgia.
✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
I used to playing with Legos. → I used to play with Legos. (Past habits: 'used to' is followed directly by the bare infinitive 'play' instead of gerund 'playing')
I wish I am a child again. → I wish I were a child again. (Hypothetical wish: use past subjunctive 'were' for present/future imaginary states)

🏁 Closing Mic Drop Rituals

1. The Final Adjective: Complete the sentence: "If I were a child again, my primary daily activity would be... [one adjective] because..."
2. The Year 2125 Cosmic Choice: Scientists invent the "Wonder Shot". It permanently resets your brain's sensory pathways so everything you see feels fresh, magical, and simple, but removes all mature, analytical logic. Would you take it, or reject it to keep adult intellect?
