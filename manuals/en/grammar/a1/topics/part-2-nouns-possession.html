Nouns & Possession

Singular and plural nouns, possessive 's, and belonging

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can describe ownership, people, places, and things using singular/plural nouns and possessive 's.

👀 What do you notice?

Notice the difference: 'teacher' (one person), 'teachers' (more than one), 'teacher's book' (book belonging to one teacher).

TypeRule / EndingExample
Regular PluralAdd -s or -esbook -> books, bus -> buses
Singular PossessiveAdd 'sSarah's car
Plural PossessiveAdd ' after -sthe girls' house

🧠 Think like a native speaker

English possessive 's replaces long preposition phrases. Instead of saying 'the car of my brother', English native speakers overwhelmingly prefer 'my brother's car'.

🗣️ Pronunciation Note

Possessive 's is pronounced /s/ after voiceless sounds (Kat's), /z/ after voiced sounds (Dog's /dɒɡz/), and /ɪz/ after sibilants (Chris's /ˈkrɪs.ɪz/).

Key examples
  • This is David's phone.
  • The children's toys are in the box.
  • Our parents' house is nearby.

💡 Memory trick

Apostrophe BEFORE s ('s) = ONE owner. Apostrophe AFTER s (s') = MANY owners!
