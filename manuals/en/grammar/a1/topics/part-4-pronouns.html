Pronoun System

Subject pronouns, object pronouns, possessive adjectives, and possessive pronouns

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can replace repetitive nouns with accurate subject, object, and possessive pronouns.

💡 Check your understanding (CCQs)

Answer these quick questions to verify meaning:

1. Sentence: "he / she / it" — Does 'She' perform the action and 'him' receive the key?
Subject pronouns perform actions; object pronouns receive actions.
2. Sentence: "he / she / it" — Do pronouns prevent repeating full nouns in conversation?
Pronouns replace nouns to keep speech concise and natural.

👀 What do you notice?

Notice how 'John saw Mary' becomes 'He saw her', and 'It is Mary's book' becomes 'It is her book' or 'It is hers'.

🧠 Think like a native speaker

English uses object pronouns after prepositions (with me, for us, to them). Never use subject pronouns after prepositions in modern English.

🗣️ Pronunciation Note

Object pronouns (me, him, her, us, them) are unstressed in normal speech: 'Give it to him' -> /ɡɪv ɪt tə ɪm/.

Key examples
  • She gave him a present.
  • Is this your coat? No, it's hers.
  • They invited us to their party.

💡 Memory trick

Subject DOES the action. Object RECEIVES the action. Possessive Adj needs a noun (my car). Possessive Pronoun stands ALONE (It is mine)!
