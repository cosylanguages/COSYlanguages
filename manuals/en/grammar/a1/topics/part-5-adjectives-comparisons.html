Adjectives & Comparisons

Describing qualities, comparative forms (-er / more), and superlative forms (-est / most)

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can describe people, places, and objects and compare them using comparative and superlative forms.

💡 Check your understanding (CCQs)

Answer these quick questions to verify meaning:

1. Sentence: "Short (1 syllable)" — Does the adjective describe a quality of the noun?
Adjectives modify nouns to describe their qualities or characteristics.
2. Sentence: "Short (1 syllable)" — Do English adjectives change ending for plural nouns (e.g. 'two new phones')?
English adjectives remain unchanged regardless of whether the noun is singular or plural.

👀 What do you notice?

Notice: 'fast' -> 'faster than' -> 'the fastest'. Notice: 'expensive' -> 'more expensive than' -> 'the most expensive'.

Adjective TypeComparative FormSuperlative Form
Short (1 syllable)-er than (taller than)the -est (the tallest)
Ending in -y-ier than (happier than)the -iest (the happiest)
Long (2+ syllables)more ... than (more modern)the most ... (the most modern)
Irregulargood -> better, bad -> worsethe best, the worst

🧠 Think like a native speaker

Adjectives in English always go BEFORE the noun ('a red car') or AFTER linking verbs ('the car is red'). Adjectives never take plural -s ('two red cars', NOT 'two reds cars').

🗣️ Pronunciation Note

The '-er' in comparatives is pronounced as a weak schwa /ər/. 'Than' is also weak: /ðən/ ('taller than' /ˈtɔː.lər ðən/).

Key examples
  • My new apartment is smaller than my old one.
  • She bought the most beautiful flowers in the market.
  • Today is better than yesterday.
⚠️ Common mistakes
❌❌ She is more taller than me.

✅✅ She is taller than me.
❌❌ This is the most fast car.
tap to see the correct form
✅✅ This is the fastest car.

💡 Memory trick

Short words add -er / -est. Long words use MORE / MOST. Never double up (don't say 'more taller')!
