Will vs Going To

Instant decisions vs pre-planned intentions

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can distinguish spontaneous decisions and offers from pre-planned intentions and evidence-based predictions.
🎯 Ready to test your skills? Reinforce this topic with interactive practice drills.
🎯 Practice this →
FormPrimary UseExample
Going ToPre-planned intentions, predictions with present evidenceI'm going to visit my grandmother this weekend. Look at those dark clouds, it's going to rain!
WillInstant decisions, promises, offers, predictions without evidenceThe phone is ringing. I'll answer it! I will help you carry those bags.

Rule: Did you decide BEFORE the moment of speaking? Use be going to. Are you deciding RIGHT NOW, offering help, or making a promise? Use will.

Examples
  • I am going to buy a new car next month (already decided).
  • I'm thirsty. I think I'll get a glass of water (spontaneous choice).
⚠️ Common mistakes
❌I decided yesterday: I will buy a laptop tomorrow.
tap to see the correct form
✅I decided yesterday: I am going to buy a laptop tomorrow.
