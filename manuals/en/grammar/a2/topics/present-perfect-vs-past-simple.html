Present Perfect vs Past Simple

Choosing the right past tense

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can choose between Past Simple and Present Perfect seamlessly based on whether time is stated.
FeaturePresent PerfectPast Simple
Timenot mentioned / not finishedfinished & often stated
Focusexperience or result nowthe event itself, in the past
Signal wordsever, never, already, just, yet, for, sinceyesterday, last week, in 2020, ago
ExampleI have visited Rome.I visited Rome in 2019.

👀 Notice: Is a time mentioned?

Ask: is there a finished time word in the sentence (yesterday, last year, in 2020)? Yes → past simple. No time, just the experience? → present perfect.

💡 Memory trick

Present Perfect + a specific time word = never together. If you can hear yourself saying when (yesterday, in 2019, last week), your verb must jump to past simple.
