Communication Question: How do I interrupt politely, hold my turn when interrupted, or invite quiet peers to speak?
System 9: Managing the Floor (Turn-Taking & Floor Control)

Managing the floor involves jumping into discussions courteously, protecting your speaking space when interrupted, and passing the microphone to others.

Why this matters: In fast group discussions or speaking club rounds, waiting silently for a pause means you never get to speak. Floor management strategies let you enter and hold turn space gracefully.
🧩 System 9 Building Blocks
Can I jump in here for a second? Just to add to that point... Let me just finish this thought... What's your take on this, [Name]? Sorry to interrupt, but...
🗺️ Floor Management Map
1. Enter Floor Interruption phrase ("Can I jump in here?")
2. Hold / Deliver Deliver point ("Just to add to that...")
3. Pass Floor Invite quiet peer ("What do you think, Sarah?")

👀 Notice

Notice how saying "Just to add to that point..." connects your interruption directly to what the previous speaker said, making the entry feel natural rather than disruptive.

🧠 Think in conversation

In group conversations, holding the floor isn't selfish; it's how turns are negotiated. Using polite floor signals ensures everyone gets heard.

🗣 Natural Speech Add-on

❌ Textbook-Stiff (Passive): [Waiting silently for 5 minutes and never getting a chance to speak]
✅ Natural Spoken (Active Entry): "Can I jump in real quick? Just to add to that..."

💡 Memory Trick

Think of floor management like passing a microphone: Ask for mic ("Can I jump in?") + Use mic ("Just adding...") + Pass mic ("What about you?").
