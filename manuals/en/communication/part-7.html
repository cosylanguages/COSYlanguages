Communication Question: How do I rephrase my idea or explain things simply when someone doesn't understand?
System 7: Explaining & Paraphrasing (Clarity & Rephrasing)

Explaining effectively involves rephrasing ideas in simpler terms and giving clear examples when listeners look confused.

Why this matters: Repeating the exact same words louder when someone doesn't understand causes frustration. Using rephrasing starters ("In other words...", "What I mean is...") simplifies the message instantly.
🧩 System 7 Building Blocks
What I mean is... In other words, ... To put it another way, ... For example, ... Does that make sense?
🗺️ Explaining & Rephrasing Map
1. State Original statement or complex idea
2. Rephrase Paraphrase starter ("In other words...") + simpler words
3. Check Check clarity ("Does that make sense?")

👀 Notice

Notice how phrases like "What I mean is..." take ownership of the explanation instead of putting pressure on the listener.

🧠 Think in conversation

Paraphrasing isn't a sign of bad grammar; it's a high-level communication skill used by native speakers to adapt to their audience.

🗣 Natural Speech Add-on

❌ Textbook-Stiff (Repetitive): "I said: It is imperative that we leave now." (Repeated verbatim)
✅ Natural Spoken (Paraphrased): "What I mean is, we really need to go right now or we'll be late."

💡 Memory Trick

Think of paraphrasing like translating into simpler language: change the wrapper, keep the gift!

💬 Mini Dialogue (Rephrasing for Clarity)

Person A: The event was rescheduled due to inclement weather.
Person B: Huh? What does that mean?
Person A: In other words, it was canceled because of heavy rain. Does that make sense?
Person B: Ah, got it! Thanks!
