Part 2 · Social Speech

Politeness, Requests & Permissions

Learn how to soften direct orders into polite requests, ask for permission, and sound warm and respectful.

💬 Communicative Goal

Avoid harsh or demanding speech (like "Give me the paper") by using polite modal openers such as "Could you please..." or "Would you mind...".

Blunt Imperative (Avoid) Polite Alternative Politeness Nuance
"Give me a pen." "Could I borrow a pen, please?" Uses modal auxiliary could to soften the request.
"Close the door." "Would you mind closing the door?" Framing as a consideration for the other person's comfort.
"I want coffee." "I'd like a coffee, please." Replaces direct want with courteous conditional would like.

💡 Pragmatic Rule of Thumb

In English, adding "Is it okay if I..." or "Do you mind if..." turns potential demands into collaborative permissions.

🔗 Cross-Manual Connections

Practice modal verbs with Book 1 Part 2 (Modal Verbs) and polite set phrases in Book 2 Part 14.

📍 Can you use this in real life?

I can use "Could you please..." to make polite requests.
I can ask for permission using "Would you mind if I...".
