Part 0 · Spoken Foundations

Foundations of Spoken English

Understand how real speech differs from textbook grammar: intonation, rhythm, fillers, and buying thinking time.

💬 Communicative Goal

Learn natural conversational habits: using hesitation tokens like "well" or "you know" instead of silence, and using active listening cues ("right", "mhm") to show engagement.

Speech Strategy Natural Expressions Function & Context
Thinking Time (Fillers) "Well...", "Let me see...", "Honestly..." Prevents unnatural silence while organizing your thoughts.
Active Listening Tokens "Right.", "I see.", "Makes sense!", "Ah, got it!" Signals to the speaker that you are following along.
Softening Starters "To be fair...", "Actually...", "I suppose..." Makes statements sound conversational rather than rigid.

💡 Pragmatic Rule of Thumb

Native speakers rarely stay completely silent while planning a sentence. Soft filler phrases like "Well, let me think..." keep your turn in the conversation open.

🔗 Cross-Manual Connections

Combine these speech fillers with basic verb structures from Book 1 (Grammar) and core adjectives from Book 2 (Vocabulary).

📍 Can you use this in real life?

I can use active listening tokens ("right", "I see") during a conversation.
