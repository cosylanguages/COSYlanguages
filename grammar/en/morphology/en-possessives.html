English — Possessive Adjectives
A1 / Beginner

Possessive adjectives show who owns or possesses something.

Why is this important? Possessive adjectives let you claim ownership and discuss other people's belongings without repeating their names. It adds clarity to everyday social interactions.
⚠ Why learners struggle: Beginners confuse pronouns (I/you) with possessive adjectives (my/your) or choose "his" or "her" based on the object's gender rather than the owner's gender.

⭐ Fully Coloured Examples
Standard sentence patterns (Between 2 and 5 examples maximum)
1st Person Singular This is my book. Belongs to me.
3rd Person Masculine It is his car. Belongs to a single male.
3rd Person Feminine This is her house. Belongs to a single female.
1st Person Plural Welcome to our home. Belongs to us.

👀 Notice

Does the possessive adjective change if the noun after it is singular ("book") or plural ("books")?

🧠 Think like English

English possessive adjectives focus entirely on the owner, not the object. If a man owns a house, a dog, and ten shirts, they are all "his" (his house, his dog, his shirts). The grammar is simple: match the possessive to the person doing the owning!

⚠ Common Mistakes
✗ Incorrect Sentence ✓ Correct Standard Sentence
John loves her car (meaning John's car). John loves his car.
This is I book. This is my book.

💡 Memory Trick

Think of possessives as sticky nametags. "His" has an "s" like standard male names (Chris, Boris), and "Her" is for she!

🔗 This prepares you for possessive pronouns like "mine", "yours", and "hers".
