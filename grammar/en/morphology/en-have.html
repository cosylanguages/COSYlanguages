English — The Verb 'To Have'
A1 / Beginner

We use "have" and "has" to show possession and describe relationships.

Why is this important? This verb is essential for talking about your family, owned belongings, and physical states. It is one of the most frequently used verbs in everyday English conversation.
⚠ Why learners struggle: Just like "to be", "to have" is irregular. It changes shape for "he, she, it" (has), which beginners frequently forget.

Standard sentence patterns (Between 2 and 5 examples maximum)
I / You / We / They We have a big house. Standard have form for plural subjects.
He / She / It He has two brothers. Has form is used for he/she/it.
I (First Person) I have a dog. Describing a pet.

👀 Notice

Which form of the verb "to have" is used when the subject is "He" or "She"?

🧠 Think like English

English verbs love changing shape for single persons (he, she, it) in the present. Think of "has" as the special compressed version of "haves" so that the solo actor stands out!

⚠ Common Mistakes
✗ Incorrect Sentence ✓ Correct Standard Sentence
He have two brothers. He has two brothers.
She hases a car. She has a car. (no double ending!)

💡 Memory Trick

Remember: He, She, and It are solo players, so they are smaller words. Therefore, they get the smaller verb HAS (3 letters) instead of HAVE (4 letters)!

🔗 This prepares you for the Present Simple negative structures using "don't have" and "doesn't have".
