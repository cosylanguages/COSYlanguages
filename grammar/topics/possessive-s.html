Part 1 &middot; Nouns & Pronouns

Whose…? Possessive ’s

Showing who owns what

📱 Vocabulary theme for practice: Family &amp; belongings
OWNERMike
’s
POSSESSIONcar
Example
one owner — 'sThis is Mike's car.
plural owners — s'The girls' bags are on the table.
question — Whose…?Whose bag is this? It's Anna's.

💡 The owner comes first

Find the owner, then add 's right after it: Mike's car. No word for 'of' is needed. Owner already ends in -s (like parents)? Just add the apostrophe: parents' house.

Rule: add 's after a singular owner (the dog's toy) and just ' after a plural noun ending in -s (my parents' house). Use whose to ask about ownership.

More examples
  • My father's car is blue.
  • The children's toys are on the floor.

💭 Think like a native speaker

The 's ending is one of the last true 'case endings' left in English — a fossil from a time when English marked grammar with endings the way Russian or German still do today.
