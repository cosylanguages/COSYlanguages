Prepositions of Time

In, on & at

PrepositionUsed forExample
inmonths, years, seasons, parts of the dayin June, in 2026, in summer, in the morning
ondays and dateson Monday, on 5th July, on my birthday
atclock times, night, short periodsat 7 o'clock, at night, at the weekend

Zoom in like a camera: wide shot (in) → medium shot (on) → close-up (at). Bigger period, bigger word first.

💡 Exceptions to remember

at night (not in the night) · the one time expression that breaks the 'in = part of day' rule.
at Christmas / Easter / the weekend · holiday periods use at, not in.
on time = punctual, exactly when planned. in time = before it's too late. Two different meanings!

🌐 Around the English-speaking world

UKAU at the weekend: See you at the weekend!

USCA on the weekend: See you on the weekend! Both are correct · just pick one and stay consistent.
