💡 Why does the verb change?

Is the subject he, she, it, or a name (like Tom)? Add -s to the verb. Any other subject (I, you, we, they)? No change at all.

💡 Memory trick

He, She, It · one person needs one extra letter: -S. Think of it as the verb wearing a name tag for a single, specific person.

Spelling of he/she/it: most verbs add -s (like → likes); after -ch,-sh,-ss,-x,-o add -es (watch → watches, go → goes); consonant+y → -ies (study → studies); have → has.

💭 Think like a native speaker

That one -s is nearly all that's left of a once-huge verb-ending system · Old English marked every person (I/you/he/we) with a different ending, the way Spanish or Russian verbs still do.

More examples

  • She works in a school.
  • Do you speak English?
