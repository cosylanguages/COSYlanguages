Part 3 &middot; Describing Things

All, Both, Either, Neither, None

Talking about a whole group, or exactly two things

📱 Vocabulary theme for practice: Groups & Family →
🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can build clear, accurate sentences and communicate confidently in everyday situations.

👀 What do you notice?

Observe the examples below. Pay attention to word order, endings, and sentence patterns before reading the rule.

Rule: all and none talk about a group (3 or more). Both, either, neither only talk about exactly two things or people.

WordGroup sizeMeaningExample
All3+every one, no exceptionsAll my friends speak English.
Both2 (only)the two togetherBoth books are mine.
Either2 (only)one or the other, your choiceYou can take either bus · they both stop here.
Neither2 (only)not the one, not the otherNeither answer is correct.
None3+not a single one, zeroNone of my friends were late.

💡 Either / neither + singular verb

Either and neither talk about two things, but the verb that follows is singular: Either day is fine. Neither answer is right.
