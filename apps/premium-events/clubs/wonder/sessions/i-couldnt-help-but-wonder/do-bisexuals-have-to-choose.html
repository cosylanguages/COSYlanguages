Do Bisexuals Have to Choose?

Exploring bisexual identity, monogamy vs sexual orientation, biphobia, and societal assumptions about romantic choice.

Duration

60 mins

Languages

English

Level

Upper Intermediate (B2/C1)

Format

Interactive Discussion

📰 PERSPECTIVE MIRROR

Does entering a long-term monogamous relationship mean choosing a side? This session examines societal assumptions around bisexuality, biphobia, fluid attraction, and personal identity. Special thanks to Cassandra Ardnassac for suggesting this thoughtful topic for our community!

📖 Session Vocabulary

Erasure /ɪˈreɪ.ʒər/

[n.]

the removal of all traces of something; social invisibility.

"Bisexual erasure occurs when media ignores fluid attraction."

≠ Antonym: Visibility

Binary /ˈbaɪ.nər.i/

[n.]

something made of two distinct parts or choices.

"Moving beyond the rigid binary allows for authentic self-expression."

≠ Antonym: Spectrum

Monogamy /məˈnɒɡ.ə.mi/

[n.]

the practice or state of being married to or in a relationship with one person at a time.

"Choosing monogamy does not alter a person's underlying orientation."

≠ Antonym: Non-monogamy

Fluidity /fluːˈɪd.ə.ti/

[n.]

the quality of being likely to change; adaptability.

"Sexual fluidity challenges rigid traditional categorizations."

≠ Antonym: Fixedness

Validation /ˌvæl.ɪˈdeɪ.ʃən/

[n.]

recognition or affirmation that a person or feeling is valid.

"Seeking social validation is natural but self-acceptance comes from within."

≠ Antonym: Rejection

Identity /aɪˈden.tə.ti/

[n.]

the fact of being who or what a person is.

"Sexual orientation is a core aspect of personal identity."

≠ Antonym: Anonymity

Dichotomy /daɪˈkɒt.ə.mi/

[n.]

a division or contrast between two things represented as opposed.

"The false dichotomy of "straight vs gay" ignores bisexual reality."

≠ Antonym: Continuum

Assumption /əˈsʌmp.ʃən/

[n.]

a thing that is accepted as true without proof.

"Societal assumptions often pressure people to pick a label."

≠ Antonym: Verified fact

Visibility /ˌvɪz.əˈbɪl.ə.ti/

[n.]

the state of being visible or noticed by the public.

"Increasing bisexual visibility helps break harmful stereotypes."

≠ Antonym: Invisibility

Stereotype /ˈster.i.ə.taɪp/

[n.]

a widely held but fixed image or idea of a particular type of person.

"Challenging outdated stereotypes builds genuine empathy."

≠ Antonym: Individual nuance

🎙️ Discussion Structure

🟠 Warm-up (10 mins)

Welcome! Share your initial thoughts on this topic. What personal experiences or observations come to mind right away?

⚡ Grammar Explanation (Concessive Clauses & Complex Contrast (even if, regardless of, despite) (B2/C1))

Use concessive clauses to express nuanced perspectives: "Regardless of who they marry, a person's orientation remains valid." / "Even if society demands simple labels, identity is complex and fluid."

🔵 Round 1: Core Exploration (Units 1–5)
1. Why does society frequently force a false dichotomy between straight and gay identities, ignoring fluidity?
★ Have you ever felt pressured by others to categorize your preferences?
2. Does entering a long-term monogamy commitment mean a bisexual person has "chosen a side"?
★ Does your relationship status define who you are as a person?
3. How does cultural erasure affect the mental health and self-acceptance of bisexual individuals?
★ How can communities create more inclusive spaces for everyone?
4. Why do harmful stereotypes about indecisiveness or infidelity persist regarding bisexuality?
★ How do you handle unexamined assumptions when meeting new people?
5. How can genuine personal validation be maintained when external social recognition is lacking?
★ Where do you find your strongest sense of self-acceptance?
🟢 Round 2: Deep Speculation & Future Horizon (Units 6–10)
6. Why is breaking free from the rigid binary essential for understanding modern romantic identity?
★ How have your views on identity evolved over the past few years?
7. How can increasing bisexual visibility in media and education counteract societal biphobia?
★ What movie or book character challenged your understanding of identity?
8. Why do people often confuse an attraction orientation with a lifestyle choice?
★ What is the difference between who you are and what choices you make daily?
9. How can partners in a relationship support each other's authentic identity without insecurity?
★ What qualities build deep trust between romantic partners?
10. What advice would you offer to someone struggling with societal pressure to label their feelings?
★ What empowering message would you share with your younger self?
📰 Introspective Speech Run

Deliver a 90-second reflection on how society can move beyond assumptions and respect personal identity.
