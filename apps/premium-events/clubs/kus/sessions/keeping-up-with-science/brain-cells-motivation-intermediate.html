Based on ScienceDaily research (August 2026). Discover the specific brain cells and neural circuits that regulate drive, perseverance, dopamine release, and goal execution.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Motivation ≠ apathy
the reason or desires that compel someone to act.
Scientists discovered brain cells that control daily motivation.
Neuron ≠ blood cell
a specialized cell transmitting nerve impulses; a nerve cell.
Specific neural circuits fire when we initiate a tough task.
Circuit ≠ fragment
a neural pathway in the brain.
Dopamine circuits regulate feelings of reward and drive.
Perseverance ≠ surrender
persistence in doing something despite difficulty.
Brain cells dedicated to perseverance help us finish long projects.
Dopamine ≠ cortisol
a neurotransmitter associated with pleasure and motivation.
Dopamine release encourages repeated goal-oriented behavior.
Discharge ≠ accumulation
the firing or release of electrical activity in neurons.
Neuronal discharge increases when a reward is near.
Incentive ≠ deterrent
a thing that motivates or encourages one to do something.
Clear incentives activate brain circuits for action.
Fatigue ≠ vigor
extreme tiredness resulting from mental or physical exertion.
Overcoming mental fatigue depends on specific cell signaling.
Stimulus ≠ inertia
a thing or event that evokes a specific functional reaction.
A positive stimulus triggers neural drive to achieve goals.
Initiative ≠ passivity
the ability to assess and initiate things independently.
Taking initiative requires strong neural activation.

🎙️ Discussion Structure

🟠 Warm-up▲
  • What is your initial reaction when reading about this scientific study?
  • How might this discovery change how we understand daily health or technology?
⚡ Grammar Practice : Relative Clauses (Which / That / Who) (B1)▲

⚡ Grammar Explanation (Intermediate (B1))

Use relative clauses to describe scientific discoveries (e.g., Scientists discovered brain cells which regulate motivation).

which that who relative clause
📝 Task 1 : Part A: Rule Formulation:

Complete the rule: Use 'who' for people, and _____ or _____ for cells, objects, and concepts.

📝 Task 1 : Part B: Structured Practice:
  1. These are the neurons _____ keep you motivated during difficult tasks.
  2. The scientist _____ led the research published the results in August.
  3. It is a discovery _____ could help treat clinical apathy.
🗣️ Task 2 : Interactive Speaking:

Describe a personal habit that keeps you motivated during work.

🔵 Round 1 : Scientific Analysis▲
How does understanding the neurons behind motivation change how we view willpower?
★ What trick do you use to start a difficult task when you lack motivation?
Why does dopamine release play such a central role in goal achievement?
★ How do you reward yourself after completing a major project?
How can recognizing neural fatigue help prevent occupational burnout?
★ How do you distinguish between physical tiredness and lack of motivation?
🟣 Let's Speak Together▲

A comparative reflection on our habits and self-perceptions.

🟢 Round 2 : Scientific Debate & Future Impact▲
Neurotech devices that artificially stimulate motivation cells will become common workplace tools. : Do you agree or disagree?
★ Would you wear a headset that boosts your focus at work?
Lack of motivation should be treated primarily as a biological issue rather than a character flaw. : Do you agree or disagree?
★ Does knowing motivation is biological change how you judge procrastination?
✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
The cells who control motivation. → The cells which / that control motivation. (Use 'which' or 'that' for non-human objects and cells, not 'who'.)
Dopamine make us feels good. → Dopamine makes us feel good. (Causative verb 'make' takes base verb without '-s': 'makes us feel'.)
