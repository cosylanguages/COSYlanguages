# Karaoke Club Pedagogical & Content Style Guide

This style guide establishes strict, non-negotiable standards for all Karaoke Club session content (English, French, Russian, Italian, Spanish, Greek) in `apps/premium-events/nights/karaoke/sessions/karaoke-club/`. It serves as the master reference for all future content creation, remediation, and automated auditing tasks.

---

## Rule 1: 100% Unique, Song-Specific Discussion Prompts (No Template Recycling)

**Hard Rule:** No sentence frame, question stem, or example sentence may be reused across two different songs in any language anywhere in the Karaoke Club catalog.

* **Concrete Lyric References Required:** Every discussion question in Stage 4 (Round 1 & Round 2) must reference a specific, verifiable element from the song's actual lyrics - such as a named character, specific location, concrete image, plot event, or direct quote.
* **Prohibition of Generic Title Slotting:** Inserting a song title or topic word into a pre-existing generic question frame (e.g. *"How does [Song Title] change how we think about [Word]?"*) is strictly prohibited.
* **Distinct Language Iterations:** Parallel localized versions of the same song must be translated faithfully while maintaining complete specificity to that song's lyrical narrative.

### Contrast Examples

| ❌ Banned Generic Pattern | ✅ Required Song-Specific Pattern |
| :--- | :--- |
| *How does the clash between technology and humanity in 'Army Dreamers' by Kate Bush change how we think about Mourning?* | *In 'Army Dreamers', Kate Bush describes a mother receiving her dead son coming home in a tin box from B.F.P.O. | *Как песня 'Волшебник-недоучка' исполнителя Алла Пугачёва раскрывает глубокое значение понятия волшебник?* | *Герой песни делает козу с одной ногой и молнию вместо грозы. Как его неловкие ошибки показывают разницу между теоретическими знаниями и реальным мастерством?* |

## Rule 2: Vocabulary Suitability & Discussion Question Qualification

**Hard Rule:** The target vocabulary words selected for discussion and highlighted in Stage 4 must be abstract concepts, character traits, emotional states, or thematic dilemmas capable of supporting personal reflection and opinion-based debate.

* **Rejection of Incidental Nouns:** Incidental physical objects, function words, body parts, or isolated surface verbs mentioned in lyrics (e.g., *coat*, *streetlights*, *bathroom*, *buttons*, *asphalte*, *tourne*) must **never** be promoted into discussion prompts or framed as philosophical topics.
* **Remediation Requirement:** If an incidental noun is listed in the song's vocabulary list:
  1. Either replace the vocabulary pick with a thematic keyword from the lyrics (e.g. replace *bathroom* with *vulnerability* or *desperation*), **or**
  2. Structure the question so that the incidental item is used strictly as a narrative anchor in the song context (e.g. *"Why does Rachel bloom with anxiety in the bathroom during the party?"*) without asking learners to reflect on their personal relationship with bathrooms.

### Banned vs. Corrected Vocabulary Framing

* ❌ **Banned:** *"Is it easy for you to share your feelings about streetlights / bathroom / buttons with others?"*
* ❌ **Banned:** *"How much do digital devices influence your daily experience of coat?"*
* ✅ **Corrected:** *"In 'Where's the Bathroom?', Paula's frantic focus on a physical facility masks her anxiety about meeting her daughter's new life. Have you ever focused on an insignificant detail when facing an awkward social situation?"*

## Rule 3: Tone & Register Alignment Matrix

**Hard Rule:** Content creators must match the emotional tone and thematic register of the actual song. High-concept trauma language must not be grafted onto lighthearted tracks, nor may triviating language be forced onto heavy, tragic songs.

### Song-Mood Register Matrix

| Song Mood Category | Typical Genres & Themes | Appropriate Question Register | Banned Phrases & Misalignments |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Comedic / Satirical** | Musical comedy (*Crazy Ex-Girlfriend*, *Heathers*), parody, kids' song pastiche. | Playful, lighthearted, humorous, examining character flaws, absurd situations, and social irony. | *"quiet burden"*, *"invisible scars"*, *"healing process"*, *"clash between technology and humanity"* |
| **Upbeat / Celebratory** | High-energy pop, dance tracks, disco (*ABBA*, *La Zarra*). | Vibrant, optimistic, focused on social connection, personal joy, ambition, and energy. | *"existential dread"*, *"trauma"*, *"silent suffering"*, *"hyper-digitized isolate"* |
| **Romantic / Idealistic** | Classic ballads, love anthems, ideal passion. | Warm, reflective on intimacy, devotion, vulnerability, and expectations in love. | *"cynical trap"*, *"corporate exploitation"*, *"digital distraction"* |
| **Heartbreak / Bittersweet** | Breakup tracks, unrequited love, nostalgic regret. | Pensive, empathetic, exploring recovery from disappointment, setting boundaries, and personal growth. | *"clash between technology and humanity"*, *"screen time habits"* |
| **Grief / Serious / War** | Anti-war anthems (*Army Dreamers*, *Casualties of War*), tragedy, bereavement. | Solemn, respectful, focused on ethical dilemmas, loss, societal responsibilities, and emotional processing. | *"digital devices"*, *"notifications"*, *"screen boundaries"*, *"online routines"* |
| **Social Critique / Protest** | Feminism, economic struggle (*Balance ton quoi*, *Don't Be a Lawyer*). | Analytical, debate-oriented, examining power dynamics, systemic expectations, and cultural norms. | Generic self-help platitudes, irrelevances like *"quiet meditation"* |
| **Nostalgic / Melancholic** | Reflective indie, retrospection (*Kate Bush*, *Zemfira*). | Poetic, introspective, focusing on memory, passing of time, personal identity, and change. | Aggressive debate-team frames, clinical jargon |
| **Introspective / Identity** | Self-discovery, psychological struggle, existential thoughts. | Nuanced, psychological, exploring inner conflict, self-acceptance, and motivation. | Cold technological framing, trivial superficiality |

## Rule 4: Strict CEFR Level Calibration & Complexity Ceiling

**Hard Rule:** Vocabulary items, grammatical structures, and discussion question complexity in Round 1 and Round 2 must strictly align with the target CEFR level of the challenge (A2, B1, B2, or C1).

* **One Sub-Level Boundary Rule:** Discussion text must not exceed the stated CEFR level by more than one sub-level (e.g., an A2 challenge may incorporate early B1 structures, but **never** B2 or C1 academic/abstract jargon).
* **Level Ceilings & Banned Vocabulary:**
  * **A2 (Elementary):** Questions must be under 15 words, using direct, everyday syntax.
    * *Banned at A2:* "inescapable", "invisible scars", "hyper-digitized", "exploit", "preservation", "existential", "redefine".
  * **B1 (Intermediate):** Clear, standard language focusing on personal opinions and familiar topics.
    * *Banned at B1:* "hyper-digitized society", "epistemological", "inexorable", "perpetuate structural oppression".
  * **B2 (Upper-Intermediate):** Complex arguments, abstract concepts, and nuanced opinions.
  * **C1 (Advanced):** Idiomatic richness, subtle stylistic nuances, and formal academic analysis.

## Rule 5: Banned "AI-Tell" Rhetorical Frames

**Hard Rule:** Text in Stage 4 must avoid the repetitive, formulaic sentence frames characteristic of machine generation. The following 10 exact overused frames are **strictly banned** from all Karaoke Club files:

1. **"How does the clash between technology and humanity in [Song] change how we think about [Word]?"**
2. **"Is [Word] a source of strength or does it expose vulnerability?"**
3. **"In what ways does [Song] illustrate that navigating a chaotic world requires a strong sense of [Word]?"**
4. **"How does [Song] capture the quiet burden / invisible scars of [Word]?"**
5. **"Do you believe that virtual interactions can ever replace face-to-face [Word]?"**
6. **"What is a digital habit or routine that you feel is actively harming your [Word]?"**
7. **"Comment [Chanson] capture-t-il le fardeau silencieux de [Mot] face aux épreuves ?"**
8. **"Pensez-vous que [Mot] est dépeint comme un piège inévitable, ou existe-t-il un chemin de guérison ?"**
9. **"Как песня [Название] раскрывает глубокое значение понятия [Слово] в нашей повседневной жизни?"**
10. **"В песне [Название] предстает ли [Слово] как источник силы или как источник сомнений?"**

## Rule 6: Contextual, Song-Specific Teacher's Notes

**Hard Rule:** The *Teacher's Note / Notes du Professeur / Заметки преподавателя* block at the end of each session must contain 3–4 realistic linguistic corrections directly inspired by the grammar, vocabulary, or pronunciation targets of **that specific song**.

* **Prohibition of Copy-Pasted Blocks:** Using identical, platform-wide default corrections (e.g., the standard *"improvement of my English"* / *"relief of hearing"* / *"in the choir since three years"* block) across multiple songs or challenges is strictly prohibited.
* **Song-Specific Alignment Requirements:**
  * **Grammar Link:** Corrections should target grammatical structures featured in the song's Stage 5 Language Focus (e.g. past modals `should have / could have` for *Army Dreamers*, second conditional for *If I Were a Rich Man*).
  * **Vocabulary Link:** Corrections should address common collocations, preposition choices, or false friends associated with the song's key lyrics.
  * **Localization:** Corrections must match the language of the session (English corrections for English songs, French for French songs, Russian for Russian songs, etc.).

    </div>
