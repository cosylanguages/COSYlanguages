❤️ Core Concepts & Feelings

Devotion
love, loyalty, or enthusiasm for a person or cause.
Their unwavering devotion shone brightest during life's darkest hour.
Opposite: Treachery
Crisis
a time of intense difficulty, trouble, or danger.
Facing a health crisis revealed who their true allies were.
Opposite: Stability
Unlikely
not likely to happen or be true; unexpected.
They formed an unlikely bond across differences and hardship.
Opposite: Expected
Sanctuary
a place of safety or quiet refuge.
Their shared hospital room became a sacred sanctuary of love.
Opposite: Danger zone
Terminal
predicted to lead to death; incurable.
A terminal diagnosis forced them to treasure every remaining hour.
Opposite: Curable

🗣️ Life, Actions & Connection

Bond
a relationship between people that has been formed.
Facing adversity forged an unbreakable bond among the four friends.
Opposite: Estrangement
Affection
a gentle feeling of fondness or liking.
Exchanging quiet affection brought warmth amidst uncertainty.
Opposite: Hostility
Solace
comfort or consolation in a time of distress.
They found solace in holding hands through the long night.
Opposite: Anguish
Adversity
a state or instance of serious or continued difficulty.
True character shines when tested by sudden adversity.
Opposite: Prosperity
Tenderness
gentleness, kindness, and affection.
Her voice softened with deep tenderness as she spoke.
Opposite: Harshness

✨ Stage 5: Language Focus▲

The Conjunction 'No matter how'

Used to express that something is true or will happen regardless of the degree or intensity of something else.

Let's Practise : Three Examples:
  • No matter how bad the weather gets, we will stand together.
  • No matter how difficult the song is, she sings it beautifully.
  • No matter how far you go, I will remember our time.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
No matter how bad is the situation, we stay together. → No matter how bad the situation is, we stay together. (Maintain standard word order after 'no matter how + adjective'.)
They are four unlikely lovers in the hospital. → They are four unlikely companions / lovers. (Use 'unlikely' as an adjectival modifier indicating unexpected pairing.)
We found solace for holding hands. → We found solace in holding hands. (Use preposition 'in + gerund' to denote the source of solace.)
