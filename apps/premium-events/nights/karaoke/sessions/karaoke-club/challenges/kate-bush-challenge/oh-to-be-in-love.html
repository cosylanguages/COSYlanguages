📚 Stage 2: Vocabulary▲

❤️ Core Concepts & Feelings

Infatuation
An intense but short-lived passion or admiration for someone or something.
Mistaking intense infatuation for mature love often leads to swift disappointment.
Euphoria
A feeling or state of intense excitement and happiness.
Stepping onto the concert stage filled the performer with overwhelming euphoria.
Vulnerable
Exposed to the possibility of being attacked or harmed, either physically or emotionally.
Opening your heart in a relationship requires being willing to feel vulnerable.
Intense
Of extreme force, degree, or strength; deeply felt.
The intense eye contact between the two actors created tangible dramatic tension.
Devotion
Love, loyalty, or enthusiasm for a person, activity, or cause.
Her steadfast devotion to her artistic craft inspired everyone in the studio.

🗣️ Life, Actions & Connection

Rapture
A feeling of intense pleasure, joy, or enthusiasm.
The singer's voice lifted the audience into a state of sheer rapture during the chorus.
Sensation
a physical feeling or perception.
The new roller coaster gives an exciting sensation of speed.
Opposite: Numbness
Bewitched
Enchanted, charmed, or put under a spell by overwhelming attraction.
He felt completely bewitched by her mysterious smile and captivating presence.
Surrender
give up or hand over after a struggle.
They decided to surrender their weapons and end the conflict.
Opposite: Resist
Heartache
Emotional anguish or grief, typically caused by unrequited love or betrayal.
Singing melancholy ballads provided a creative outlet for her lingering heartache.

💬 Stage 4: Discussion▲

🔵 Round 1 — Lyric Analysis & Discussion

Kate Bush describes a sudden spark where past memory becomes "terribly vague" as the light hits someone moving across the floor. How does early Infatuation blur our awareness of everything that came before?
★ When did you last experience a sudden wave of Infatuation that made you completely forget what you were doing?
In the chorus, the exclamation "Oh, to be in love and never get out again!" captures a dizzying sense of romantic Euphoria. Why do we crave staying trapped in this feeling forever?
★ What simple moment or song instantly triggers a feeling of pure Euphoria for you?
The singer asks "Why did you have to make me feel that?", realizing how open and Vulnerable she has suddenly become. Is falling in love scary because it strips away our emotional defenses?
★ Do you find it easy or intimidating to allow yourself to feel Vulnerable when new feelings begin?
The lyric "I could have been anyone, you could have been anyone's dream" highlights how a chance encounter suddenly feels wildly Intense and meaningful. How does love turn random coincidences into magic?
★ Have you ever had a chance meeting or conversation that felt unexpectedly Intense?
When Kate Bush sings "Everything they say seems to sound new," new love inspires a playful, eager Devotion that turns everyday words into poetry. How does being in love change how we listen to people?
★ What is a hobby, passion, or person in your life that receives your unshakeable Devotion?
The line "All the colours look brighter now" vividly conveys the physical and sensory Rapture of falling head over heels. Why does falling in love seem to alter our actual perception of the world?
★ What visual or sensory experience always fills you with a sense of wonder and Rapture?
With time "slipping into tomorrow too quick," the singer experiences the dizzying Sensation of time speeding up during perfect moments. Why does time feel so elastic when we are happy?
★ Have you ever felt the strange Sensation of a whole day flying by in what felt like minutes?
When she pleads "Stop the swing of the pendulum, let us through!", the singer is so Bewitched that she wants to pause time itself. What makes us wish we could freeze a moment in place?
★ What is a place or memory that has left you completely Bewitched and wishing time would stand still?
Repeating "Oh, to be in love and never get out again" represents a joyful Surrender to the chaos of love rather than a struggle for control. Why is giving up control so liberating in romantic relationships?
★ In what situations in life do you find it refreshing to Surrender control and just go with the flow?
Although 'Oh To Be in Love' is overflowing with joy, the fear that yesterday was "always too good to forget" hints at how quickly sweetness can turn to Heartache if the spell breaks. How does the joy of love carry a tiny shadow of potential heartbreak?
★ How do you protect your sense of joy while navigating past disappointments or Heartache?

🟢 Round 2 — Broad Themes & Philosophy Debate

The dizzying Euphoria and Infatuation described in 'Oh To Be in Love' is not true love, but rather a temporary chemical illusion that distorts reality. Let's debate this perspective.
Wishing to "stop the swing of the pendulum" and remain frozen in romantic Rapture forever is a natural human desire, not a naive fantasy. Share your thoughts.
Experiencing love so deeply that all "colours look brighter" requires us to Surrender our emotional self-control completely. Defend or oppose this.
✨ Stage 5: Language Focus▲

Language Focus: Infinitive Exclamations & Adjectives

In the song Oh To Be In Love, the repetitive lyric chunk "Oh to be in love... and close to you" highlights the use of Infinitive Exclamations & Adjectives. This structure supports: Celebrating new romantic feelings and joy.

Let's Practise — Three Examples:
  • Practice using "Oh to be in love... and close to you" or related forms actively during your discussion rounds today.
  • Notice how the lyric chunk "Oh to be in love... and close to you" builds rhythmic familiarity and emotional weight.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
Oh to be in love and never get off again → Oh to be in love and never get out again (Use 'get out' (of a state/situation) rather than 'get off' when describing remaining trapped in love.)
All the colours look more bright now → All the colours look brighter now (Use single-syllable comparative adjectives ending in '-er' ('brighter') rather than 'more bright'.)
You could have been be anyone's dream → You could have been anyone's dream (Use past modal 'could have + past participle' ('been') to express past possibility.)
