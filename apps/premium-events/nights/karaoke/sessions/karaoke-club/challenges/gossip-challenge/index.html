casualties
Persons killed or injured in a war or accident, or victims of emotional conflict.

war
having or showing a moderate degree of heat.

battles
Sustained fights between opposing forces, or difficult personal struggles.

scars
a mark left on the skin or within a person's mind.

fighting
the action of defeating or resisting something; struggling.
Fighting for social justice requires courage and perseverance.
Opposite: surrendering

strength

Opposite: weakness
pain
highly distressing physical or emotional sensation.

survive
continue to live or exist in spite of danger.

Opposite: perish
peace

Language Focus: Present Participles as Durative Modifiers

In the song Casualties Of War, the repetitive lyric chunk "We're casualties of war... carrying the scars" highlights the use of Present Participles as Durative Modifiers. This structure supports: Describing lingering inner struggles and trauma.

Expressing Modals of Possibility ('might / would')

In English, 'might' expresses a possibility or risk in the present or future, while 'would' or 'would rather' expresses preference or hypothethicals.

Let's Practise — Three Examples:

  • I would rather be alone than spend sleepless nights awake wonderin'.
  • Romance might be a blur, but our connection feels very real.
