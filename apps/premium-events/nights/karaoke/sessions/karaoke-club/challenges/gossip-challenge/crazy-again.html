📚 Stage 2: Vocabulary▲

❤️ Core Concepts & Feelings

romance
A feeling of excitement and mystery associated with love.
Rekindling romance requires continuous effort and thoughtful gestures.
Opposite: indifference
savior
A person who rescues another from harm, danger, or failure.
Expecting a romantic partner to act as your ultimate savior sets unrealistic demands.
Opposite: enemy
crazy
Mentally deranged, wild, or wildly eccentric and passionate.
Falling wildly in love again made her act in unpredictable and passionate ways.
Opposite: sane
obligation
An act or course of action to which a person is morally or legally bound.
Fulfilling family obligation out of love is rewarding, but out of fear it is exhausting.
Opposite: freedom
fragile
Easily broken, damaged, or vulnerable to emotional distress.
Her newly restored confidence felt fragile in the presence of harsh criticism.
Opposite: tough

✨ Stage 5: Language Focus▲

Expressing Modals of Possibility ('might / would')

In English, 'might' expresses a possibility or risk in the present or future, while 'would' or 'would rather' expresses preference or hypothethicals.

Let's Practise — Three Examples:
  • I might go crazy if I spend another lonely evening here.
  • I would rather be alone than spend sleepless nights awake wonderin'.
  • Romance might be a blur, but our connection feels very real.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
There is a big improvement of my English → There is a big improvement in my English (Use 'in' after 'improvement' to express domain.)
It was a great relief of hearing the news → It was a great relief to hear the news (Use 'relief' + infinitive 'to hear'.)
She is in the choir since three years → She has been in the choir for three years (Use present perfect + 'for' to show duration of state.)
