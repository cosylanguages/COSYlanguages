📚 Stage 2: Vocabulary▲

❤️ Core Concepts & Feelings

motivation
The reason or reasons one has for acting or behaving in a particular way.
Uncovering your true inner motivation helps maintain focus during tough projects.
Opposite: apathy
direction
a course along which someone or something moves, or guidance.
The signposts provided clear direction through the complex maze.
Opposite: chaos
tears
drops of clear salty liquid secreted from the eyes when crying.
Tears of joy streamed down her face when she won the championship.
Opposite: smiles
fears
Plural of fear; plural unpleasant emotions caused by perceived danger or threat.
Confronting your deepest fears helps clear the path toward authentic self-acceptance.
Opposite: courage
reminiscing
Indulging in enjoyable recollection of past events.
Reminiscing about late-night conversations brought smiles to their faces.
Opposite: forgetting

✨ Stage 5: Language Focus▲

Expressing Direction and Intent using 'Move in'

To convey determined forward progress, English uses action verbs with 'in' or 'forward'. Contrast this with words of regret or hesitation.

Let's Practise — Three Examples:
  • I want to move in the right direction and leave my fears behind.
  • Hesitation was my first instinct, but now I feel strong.
  • Keeping my head up helps me look forward to tomorrow.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
There is a big improvement of my English → There is a big improvement in my English (Use 'in' after 'improvement' to express domain.)
It was a great relief of hearing the news → It was a great relief to hear the news (Use 'relief' + infinitive 'to hear'.)
She is in the choir since three years → She has been in the choir for three years (Use present perfect + 'for' to show duration of state.)
