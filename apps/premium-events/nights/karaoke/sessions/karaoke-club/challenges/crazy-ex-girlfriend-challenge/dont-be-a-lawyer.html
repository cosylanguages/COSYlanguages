📚 Stage 2: Vocabulary▲

❤️ Core Concepts & Feelings

Lucrative
producing a great deal of profit; profitable.
He decided to leave his low-paying job to pursue a more lucrative career in tech.
Opposite: Unprofitable
Prestigious
inspiring respect and admiration; having high status.
Glendale University is not exactly a prestigious Ivy League school.
Opposite: Obscure
Vocation
a strong feeling of suitability for a particular career or occupation; a calling.
She always felt that teaching was her true vocation, not just a way to pay the bills.
Opposite: Chore
Hesitation
the action of pausing or hesitating before saying or doing something.
She accepted the job offer without a moment's hesitation.
Opposite: Alacrity
Ruin
to damage or destroy something completely.
Working eighty hours a week is the quickest way to ruin your mental health.
Opposite: Restore

🗣️ Life, Actions & Connection

Inherently
in a permanent, essential, or characteristic way.
Many people believe that corporate law is inherently stressful and unhappy.
Opposite: Superficially
Debt
something, typically money, that is owed or due.
Going to law school left him with a mountain of student debt.
Opposite: Asset
Regret
feel sad, repentant, or disappointed over.
He expressed deep regret for his past mistakes.
Opposite: Satisfaction
Expertise
expert skill or knowledge in a particular field.
His primary expertise lies in tax law and small business consulting.
Opposite: Incompetence
Avoid
to keep away from or stop oneself from doing something.
If you want to be happy, you should avoid toxic work environments.
Opposite: Confront

💬 Stage 4: Discussion▲

🔵 Round 1 — Lyric Analysis & Discussion

The song warns against choosing a career path just because it seems Lucrative. Why do people often prioritize money over passion?
★ If you won the lottery tomorrow, would you still continue in your current field, or do something less Lucrative?
The protagonist was accepted into Glendale U because it was a 'safe bet' for a Prestigious vocation. Does prestige guarantee job satisfaction?
★ Have you ever felt pressure from your parents or peers to attend a Prestigious institution or choose a specific high-status job?
Jim says he became an attorney 'without a moment's hesitation' because it seemed like a solid Vocation. What is your true calling?
★ Do you believe that everyone has one specific Vocation, or can we find fulfillment in many different careers?
Rushing into a huge career choice with too much Hesitation can lead to deep regret. How can we make better life choices?
★ Tell us about a time you made a major decision without any Hesitation. Did it turn out well?
The lyrics advise that corporate law is the quickest way to Ruin your life. How does overworking affect our personal lives?
★ What is one bad habit or stressor that has the potential to Ruin a perfectly good weekend for you?
The singer claims the job of an attorney is Inherently crappy. What makes a work environment inherently unhappy?
★ Do you think that high-stress jobs are Inherently bad, or does it depend entirely on the person's attitude and coping skills?
Law school is notorious for leaving graduates with massive student Debt. Is a high-status degree worth the financial burden?
★ What is the best financial advice you've ever received for managing or avoiding personal Debt?
The song contrasts daily professional Regret with simple, happy trades like tailoring. Why do simple jobs bring so much peace?
★ What is one choice in your past that you initially questioned, but now look back on with absolutely zero Regret?
Jim jokes that your only Expertise as a lawyer is 'runnin' up fees and speakin' legalese.' What is your main professional strength?
★ Is there a specific skill or area of Expertise you would love to master in the next five years?
The song tells us that it is never too late to Avoid this unhappy fate. How can someone successfully pivot their career?
★ What is a toxic type of conversation or situation at work that you always try your best to Avoid?
🎵 Interactive Musical Checkpoints (Karaoke Identity)
  • Before Listening: Predict based on the title 'Don't Be a Lawyer': Do you think this song is an intense, dramatic warning, or a lighthearted, fast-paced parody?
  • After Verse 1 (Pause & Predict): Pause after Jim says he got accepted at Glendale U. What kind of future is he setting up? Do you expect him to be happy or miserable?
  • After the first Chorus (Rhythmic Delivery): How does the fast, energetic rhythm and the backup dancers' enthusiasm contrast with the extremely negative advice being given?
  • During the Specialty Law QA (Musical Comedy): Listen to the section where characters ask about 'human rights law', 'environmental law', and 'immigration law'. Why does Jim keep repeating 'No money, plus it's a bummer'?
  • After Singing/Listening (Lyrical Takeaway): Which specific warning or joke in the song made you laugh or think the most? Why?

🟢 Round 2 — Broad Themes & Philosophy Debate

Pursuing a highly Lucrative career is pointless if the daily work is inherently miserable. Agree or disagree?
Society places too much value on Prestigious titles (like doctor, lawyer, or executive) instead of actual happiness. Agree or disagree?
Most people don't find their true Vocation until they are in their thirties or forties. Agree or disagree?
Taking a year off to travel or reflect is a sign of wisdom, not lazy Hesitation. Agree or disagree?
Working eighty hours a week will eventually Ruin any personal relationship. Agree or disagree?
No job is Inherently perfect; satisfaction is entirely what you make of it. Agree or disagree?
Going into heavy financial Debt for education is a sensible investment in your future. Agree or disagree?
It is far better to change your path late in life than to live with the Regret of what could have been. Agree or disagree?
In the modern job market, having broad adaptability is more valuable than highly narrow technical Expertise. Agree or disagree?
The best way to Avoid professional burnout is to strictly separate your job from your personal identity. Agree or disagree?

✨ Stage 5: Language Focus▲

Imperative Verbs & Modals of Strong Advice ('should / must / ought to')

The song uses direct imperatives (e.g., 'Don't be a lawyer!') to express powerful warnings. Combined with modals of strong advice like 'should', 'must', and 'ought to', we can construct compelling, persuasive arguments about life, work, and following one's passion.

Let's Practise — Three Examples:
  • Don't study law unless you are prepared to spend eighty hours a week workin' on pharmaceutical mergers.
  • You should avoid choosing a lucrative career if it inherently ruins your peace of mind.
  • We must listen to our true vocation instead of rushing into decisions with too much hesitation.
