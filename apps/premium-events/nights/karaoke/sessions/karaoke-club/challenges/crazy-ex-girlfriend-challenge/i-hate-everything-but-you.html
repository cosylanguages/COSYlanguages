📚 Stage 2: Vocabulary▲

❤️ Core Concepts & Feelings

crowded
full of people, leaving little or no room.
The popular beach was very crowded on the warm summer weekend.
Opposite: empty
politician
a person who is professionally involved in politics, especially as a holder of an elected office.
The local politician promised to improve public parks and libraries.
smiling
having a smile on one's face, showing pleasure or affection.
The smiling teacher greeted her new class with immense enthusiasm.
place
a particular position, point, or area.
The quiet park near her house is her favorite place to read books.
everything
all things, or all things of a particular class.
They lost everything in the fire but were grateful to be safe.

🗣️ Life, Actions & Connection

traffic
Vehicles moving on public roads or communication congestion.
Sitting in heavy gridlock traffic can test anyone's patience during a busy morning commute.
rain
Water falling in drops from clouds in the atmosphere.
The sudden cold rain soaked her clothes, matching her gloomy and frustrated mood.
hate
Feel intense dislike or aversion toward someone or something.
She claimed to hate everything about the town, except for the one person who captured her heart.
greedy
having or showing an intense and selfish desire for something, especially wealth or power.
The greedy corporation prioritizes short-term profits over safety.
beautiful
pleasing the senses or mind aesthetically.
The sunset over the mountain was one of the most beautiful sights she had ever seen.

Language Focus: Indefinite Pronouns with Exceptive *But*

In the song I Hate Everything But You, the repetitive lyric chunk "I hate everything but you" highlights the use of Indefinite Pronouns with Exceptive *But*. This structure supports: Formulating extreme selective affection.

There is a big improvement of my English → There is a big improvement in my English (Use 'in' after 'improvement' to express domain.)
It was a great relief of hearing the news → It was a great relief to hear the news (Use 'relief' + infinitive 'to hear'.)
She is in the choir since three years → She has been in the choir for three years (Use present perfect + 'for' to show duration of state.)
