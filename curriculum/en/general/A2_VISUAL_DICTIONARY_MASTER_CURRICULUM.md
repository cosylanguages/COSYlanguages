## PART 1 — PERSONAL LIFE, RELATIONSHIPS & CHARACTER

### UNIT 1: Detailed Personal Profiles & Life History (A2.1)
- **1.1 Life History, Upbringing & Biography**
  - **Core Vocabulary:** Biography, background, birthplace, hometown, upbringing, childhood, youth, adulthood, memory, milestone, life event, degree, graduation, qualification, milestone, transition.
  - **Essential Verbs:** Be born, grow up, move, relocate, graduate, qualify, start work, retire, pass away, remember, recall, adapt, adjust.
  - **Adjectives:** Memorable, unforgettable, early, late, major, significant, gradual, peaceful, challenging.
  - **Collocations:** Early childhood, family background, graduate from university, move house, career milestone, cherish a memory.
  - **Set Phrases:** Where were you born? / I was born in 1998 in a small coastal town. / I grew up in Chicago before moving to London. / When did you graduate? / He moved abroad three years ago.
  - **Sentence Frames:** `I grew up in + [Location] + with + [Family]`, `After graduating from + [Institution], I moved to + [Location]`.
  - **Micro-Dialogue:**
    - *A:* Where did you grow up, Marcus?
    - *B:* I grew up in Manchester, but my family moved to London when I was twelve.
    - *A:* How was the transition for you?
    - *B:* It was challenging at first, but I adapted quickly and made new friends.

- **1.2 Personality Traits, Temperament & Character**
  - **Core Vocabulary:** Personality, character, trait, quality, flaw, temperament, attitude, sense of humour, mood, nature, behavior.
  - **Adjectives (Positive & Negative):** Reliable, hard-working, lazy, generous, selfish, patient, impatient, stubborn, flexible, moody, cheerful, shy, outgoing, polite, rude, honest, dishonest, sensitive, sensible, confident, modest, ambitious, talkative, quiet.
  - **Collocations:** Warm personality, reliable friend, hard-working employee, stubborn attitude, great sense of humour, change in mood.
  - **Set Phrases:** What is she like? / She is very hard-working and reliable. / He can be a bit stubborn at times. / She has a fantastic sense of humour. / Don't be so impatient!
  - **Sentence Frames:** `He strikes me as + [Adjectives]`, `She is usually + [Trait], but sometimes she can be + [Trait]`.
  - **Micro-Dialogue:**
    - *A:* What is your new manager like?
    - *B:* She's very polite and sensible, but she expects everyone to be hard-working and punctual.
    - *A:* Sounds like a fair boss!

## PART 1 — TENSES & ASPECT IN DEPTH (A2.1 & A2.2)

### UNIT 1: Past Simple vs. Past Continuous
- **Structural Formula:**
  - *Past Simple:* `Subject + Verb-ed / Irregular Past` (*I walked / He wrote*).
  - *Past Continuous:* `Subject + was/were + Verb-ing` (*I was walking / They were writing*).
- **Core Rules & Usage:**
  - Past Simple is used for completed actions in the past.
  - Past Continuous is used for ongoing background actions in progress in the past.
  - Interrupted actions: `Past Continuous (ongoing background) + WHEN + Past Simple (short interrupting action)` OR `Past Simple + WHILE + Past Continuous`.
- **Examples:**
  - *I was sleeping when the alarm went off.*
  - *While we were driving to the airport, it started to rain heavily.*
  - *She was writing an email when her computer crashed.*
- **Common Mistakes:**
  - ❌ *I slept when she called me.* ➔ ✔️ *I was sleeping when she me called.*
  - ❌ *While I was going home, I was seeing a car accident.* ➔ ✔️ *While I was going home, I saw a car accident.*
- **Mini Dialogue:**
  - *A:* What were you doing when the lights went out last night?
  - *B:* I was cooking dinner in the kitchen.

### UNIT 2: Present Perfect Simple (Experience, Duration & Recent Events)
- **Structural Formula:**
  - `Subject + have/has + Past Participle (V3)`
- **Core Rules & Usage:**
  - 1. **Life Experiences:** Using *ever* (questions) and *never* (statements) without specifying exact time. (*Have you ever visited Rome? I have never tried sushi.*)
  - 2. **Duration from Past to Present:** Using *for* (duration: 3 years, 5 hours) and *since* (starting point: 2018, Monday, I was a child). (*I have lived in London for five years. She has worked here since 2020.*)
  - 3. **Recent Events & Results:** Using *just* (very recent), *already* (sooner than expected), and *yet* (questions/negatives). (*I have just finished my homework. Has the train arrived yet? He hasn't called yet.*)
- **Contrast: Present Perfect vs. Past Simple:**
  - *Present Perfect:* Time is unfinished / unstated (*I have been to Paris*).
  - *Past Simple:* Time is specified and completed (*I went to Paris in 2022*).
- **Common Mistakes:**
  - ❌ *I live here since three years.* ➔ ✔️ *I have lived here for three years.*
  - ❌ *I have seen him yesterday.* ➔ ✔️ *I saw him yesterday.*
- **Mini Dialogue:**
  - *A:* Have you ever eaten Thai food?
  - *B:* Yes, I have! I ate it last week at a new restaurant downtown.

### UNIT 3: Future Tenses Contrast (Will vs. Be Going To vs. Present Continuous)
- **Structural System & Differences:**
  - 1. **Be Going To (`Subject + am/is/are going to + Base Verb`):** Prior plans, intentions, and predictions based on present visual evidence. (*I am going to buy a car next month. Look at those dark clouds, it's going to rain!*)
  - 2. **Present Continuous (`Subject + am/is/are + Verb-ing`):** Fixed future arrangements with specific time/place/people. (*I am meeting the doctor at 3 PM tomorrow.*)
  - 3. **Will (`Subject + will + Base Verb`):** Instant decisions made at the moment of speaking, offers, promises, and general predictions. (*It's cold, I will close the window. I will help you with those bags.*)
- **Common Mistakes:**
  - ❌ *Tomorrow I will meet my dentist at 10 AM.* (It's a fixed appointment) ➔ ✔️ *Tomorrow I am meeting my dentist at 10 AM.*
- **Mini Dialogue:**
  - *A:* What are your plans for the weekend?
  - *B:* I am going to visit my grandparents on Saturday.

## PART 2 — MODALS & EXPRESSING OBLIGATION / PERMISSION

### UNIT 4: Obligation & Necessity (Have to / Don't have to / Must / Mustn't)
- **Structural System:**
  - `Have to / Has to + Base Verb`: External rule, law, or situational necessity. (*Drivers have to wear seatbelts.*)
  - `Don't have to / Doesn't have to + Base Verb`: Lack of obligation / optional action (*You don't have to wear a suit, it's casual.*)
  - `Must + Base Verb`: Personal felt obligation or strict rule/written notice. (*I must call my mother tonight.*)
  - `Mustn't + Base Verb`: Prohibition / forbidden action! (*You mustn't smoke in the hospital!*)
- **Critical Distinction:** *Don't have to* means optional; *Mustn't* means illegal or forbidden!
- **Common Mistakes:**
  - ❌ *You mustn't pay for the museum, it's free!* ➔ ✔️ *You don't have to pay for the museum, it's free!*

### UNIT 5: Advice & Recommendations (Should / Shouldn't)
- **Structural System:** `Subject + should / shouldn't + Base Verb`
- **Usage:** Giving friendly advice, recommendations, or opinions.
- **Examples:**
  - *You look tired; you should go to bed early tonight.*
  - *You shouldn't drink so much coffee before sleep.*

### UNIT 6: Polite Requests, Permission & Ability (Could / Would you mind / May)
- **Structural System:**
  - *Could you + Base Verb... ?* (*Could you pass the salt, please?*)
  - *Would you mind + Verb-ing... ?* (*Would you mind opening the window?*)
  - *May I + Base Verb... ?* (*May I use your bathroom?*)

## PART 3 — CONDITIONALS & COMPARISONS

### UNIT 7: Zero & First Conditionals
- **Zero Conditional (General Truths & Scientific Facts):**
  - `IF + Present Simple, Present Simple`
  - *If you heat ice, it melts.* / *If water reaches 100 degrees, it boils.*
- **First Conditional (Real Future Possibilities & Consequences):**
  - `IF + Present Simple, WILL + Base Verb`
  - *If it rains tomorrow, we will stay at home.*
  - *If you study hard, you will pass the exam.*
- **Common Mistakes:**
  - ❌ *If it will rain tomorrow, we will stay inside.* ➔ ✔️ *If it rains tomorrow, we will stay inside.*

### UNIT 8: Second Conditional Introduction (Hypothetical Situations)
- **Structural System:**
  - `IF + Past Simple, WOULD + Base Verb`
- **Usage:** Imagined, unreal, or unlikely situations in the present or future.
- **Examples:**
  - *If I had more free time, I would learn how to play the guitar.*
  - *If I won the lottery, I would buy a house by the sea.*
  - *If I were you, I would talk to the manager.* (Giving advice with *if I were you*)

### UNIT 9: Advanced Comparatives & Superlatives
- **Modifiers for Degree:**
  - *Much / A lot / Far + Comparative:* `much more expensive`, `a lot cheaper`, `far better`.
  - *A bit / Slightly + Comparative:* `a bit warmer`, `slightly larger`.
- **Structures of Equality & Excess:**
  - `As + Adjective + As`: *This car is as fast as that one.*
  - `Too + Adjective + Infinitive`: *It is too cold to swim today.*
  - `Not + Adjective + Enough + Infinitive`: *He isn't tall enough to play basketball.*

## PART 4 — CLAUSES, CONNECTORS & REPORTED SPEECH

### UNIT 10: Defining Relative Clauses (Who, Which, That, Where, Whose)
- **Usage:** Adding essential information to identify a person, thing, place, or possessor.
- **Relative Pronouns:**
  - *Who / That* (for people): *The woman who lives next door is a doctor.*
  - *Which / That* (for things): *This is the book which I borrowed from the library.*
  - *Where* (for places): *That is the restaurant where we had dinner.*
  - *Whose* (for possession): *I met a man whose brother works at NASA.*

### UNIT 11: Discourse Connectors & Conjunctions
- **Contrast:** *Although / Even though + clause*, *However, [sentence]*, *But*.
  - *Although it was raining, we went for a walk.*
  - *The hotel was expensive. However, the service was excellent.*
- **Reason & Result:** *Because + clause*, *Because of + noun*, *So + clause*.
  - *We stayed inside because it was raining.*
  - *We stayed inside because of the heavy rain.*
  - *It was raining, so we stayed inside.*

### UNIT 12: Basic Reported Speech (Statements & Backshift)
- **Core Rules:** Reporting what someone said in the past requires shifting tenses back one step into the past.
- **Tense Shifts:**
  - *Present Simple ➔ Past Simple:* "I am tired" ➔ *He said he was tired.*
  - *Present Continuous ➔ Past Continuous:* "I am working" ➔ *She said she was working.*
  - *Can ➔ Could:* "I can swim" ➔ *He said he could swim.*
  - *Will ➔ Would:* "I will call you" ➔ *She said she would call me.*
- **Reporting Verbs:** `Say (that)` vs `Tell (someone) (that)`.
  - *He said that he was busy.* (No object after *say*)
  - *He told me that he was busy.* (Direct object after *tell*)
