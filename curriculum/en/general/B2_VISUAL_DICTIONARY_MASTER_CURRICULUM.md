## PART 1 — SOCIETY, POLITICS, LAW & ENVIRONMENT

### UNIT 1: Global Issues, Climate Action & Sustainability (B2.1)
- **1.1 Climate Change, Energy Transition & Carbon Neutrality**
  - **Core Vocabulary:** Greenhouse gas emissions, global warming, carbon footprint, carbon neutrality, fossil fuel reliance, renewable energy transition, eco-system collapse, biodiversity loss, environmental conservation, sustainability initiative, renewable grid, solar irradiance, geothermal energy.
  - **Essential Verbs:** Mitigate, phase out, offset, deplete, transition, implement, advocate, enforce, conserve, jeopardize.
  - **Adjectives:** Carbon-neutral, sustainable, eco-friendly, catastrophic, irreversible, finite, viable, renewable, depleting.
  - **Collocations:** Mitigate climate change, phase out fossil fuels, carbon offset scheme, irreversible damage, renewable energy grid, achieve carbon neutrality, finite resources.
  - **Set Phrases:** Achieving carbon neutrality requires global cooperation and political determination. / We must drastically reduce our reliance on fossil fuels to avert environmental crisis. / The government has committed to phasing out coal power by 2030.
  - **Sentence Frames:** `The failure to mitigate + [Issue] + will inevitably lead to + [Consequence]`, `To achieve + [Target], industries must transition towards + [Solution]`.
  - **Micro-Dialogue:**
    - *A:* What is your perspective on the new environmental policy proposed by the ministry?
    - *B:* While phasing out fossil fuels is essential, the government must provide incentives for companies to transition to renewable energy grids without disrupting employment.

- **1.2 Social Justice, Law & Human Rights**
  - **Core Vocabulary:** Legislation, human rights, systemic discrimination, social equality, justice, judicial system, legal reform, civil liberties, compliance, enforcement, jurisprudence, trial, defense, prosecution.
  - **Essential Verbs:** Enact, uphold, violate, reform, prosecute, litigate, safeguard, advocate, discriminate.
  - **Adjectives:** Judicial, systemic, unconstitutional, binding, arbitrary, equitable, legislative.
  - **Collocations:** Enact legislation, uphold human rights, systemic discrimination, landmark legal reform, constitutional rights, binding agreement, fair trial.
  - **Set Phrases:** Everyone is entitled to a fair trial under the rule of law. / Comprehensive legal reforms are required to address systemic inequalities. / The legislation was passed to safeguard civil liberties.
  - **Micro-Dialogue:**
    - *A:* How will the new data protection law impact international corporations?
    - *B:* It creates a legally binding framework that forces companies to uphold strict user privacy rights, or face severe financial penalties.

### UNIT 2: Culture, Cinema & Literary Analysis (B2.2)
- **2.1 Film Deconstruction & Screenplay Analysis**
  - **Core Vocabulary:** Protagonist, antagonist, character arc, conflict, climax, resolution, cinematography, screenplay, subtext, motif, genre, visual symbolism, pacing, director's vision.
  - **Essential Verbs:** Depict, foreshadow, deconstruct, convey, evoke, symbolize, critique, mirror.
  - **Adjectives:** Compelling, evocative, melodramatic, subtle, poignant, visceral, thought-provoking.
  - **Collocations:** Compelling character arc, underlying subtext, visual motif, evocative cinematography, dramatic climax, deconstruct a scene.
  - **Set Phrases:** The protagonist undergoes a profound character arc throughout the second act. / The cinematography subtly mirrors the emotional isolation of the character. / Subtext plays a crucial role in the dialogue.
  - **Micro-Dialogue:**
    - *A:* What did you make of the movie's ambiguous ending?
    - *B:* I thought it was brilliant! The director deliberately used subtle visual motifs to leave the resolution open to individual interpretation.

- **2.2 Literary Criticism & Argumentative Rhetoric**
  - **Core Vocabulary:** Narrative structure, metaphor, allegory, symbolism, premise, thesis, argument, counter-argument, rebuttal, evidence, persuasive device, satire, tone.
  - **Essential Verbs:** Assert, refute, illustrate, symbolize, elaborate, critique, challenge, substantiate.
  - **Adjectives:** Persuasive, flawed, coherent, nuanced, metaphorical, satirical, compelling.
  - **Collocations:** Formulate a thesis, present a compelling argument, convincing rebuttal, substantiating evidence, literary satire.
  - **Set Phrases:** The novel serves as an allegory for modern political corruption. / Her argument was supported by rigorous empirical evidence. / The author refutes the traditional narrative structure.
  - **Micro-Dialogue:**
    - *A:* How did the essay address the counter-arguments?
    - *B:* The author presented a thorough rebuttal, citing historical evidence to substantiate her primary thesis.

## PART 1 — ADVANCED TENSES & CONDITIONALS (B2.1 & B2.2)

### UNIT 1: Mixed Conditionals (Past Cause ➔ Present Result & Vice Versa)
- **Type 1: Past Cause ➔ Present Result:**
  - `IF + Past Perfect, WOULD + Base Verb`
  - Used to describe how an unfulfilled past event affects a present situation.
  - *If I had accepted that job offer in New York last year, I would be living in Manhattan right now.*
  - *If she had taken her doctor's advice, she wouldn't be feeling so ill today.*
- **Type 2: Present State ➔ Past Result:**
  - `IF + Past Simple (State), WOULD HAVE + Past Participle (V3)`
  - Used to describe how a permanent present trait/state affected a past event.
  - *If I were more confident, I would have spoken up during yesterday's meeting.*
  - *If he spoke fluent French, he would have been selected for the Paris assignment.*
- **Common Mistakes:**
  - ❌ *If I accepted that job last year, I would be in New York now.* ➔ ✔️ *If I had accepted that job last year, I would be in New York now.*

### UNIT 2: Future Perfect & Future Continuous
- **Future Continuous (`Subject + will be + Verb-ing`):**
  - Action that will be in progress at a specific moment in the future.
  - *This time tomorrow, I will be presenting our proposal to the board.*
  - *Don't call at 8 PM; we will be having dinner.*
- **Future Perfect Simple (`Subject + will have + Past Participle (V3)`):**
  - Action that will be completed BEFORE a specific point in the future.
  - *By the end of this decade, scientists will have developed new clean energy solutions.*
  - *By 5 PM, I will have finished writing the entire specification.*
- **Future Perfect Continuous (`Subject + will have been + Verb-ing`):**
  - Emphasizing the continuous duration of an action up to a future point.
  - *By next month, I will have been working at this firm for ten years.*

### UNIT 3: Inversion for Emphasis & Formal Tone
- **Rules:** When negative or restrictive adverbials are placed at the beginning of a sentence for dramatic emphasis or formal written style, the auxiliary verb and subject invert (`Adverbial + Auxiliary Verb + Subject + Main Verb`).
- **Key Negative / Restrictive Adverbials:**
  - *Hardly / Scarcely... when:* *Hardly had I entered the room when the alarm sounded.*
  - *No sooner... than:* *No sooner had the conference started than the fire alarm went off.*
  - *Rarely / Seldom:* *Rarely have I witnessed such exceptional leadership.*
  - *Under no circumstances:* *Under no circumstances should you share confidential data.*
  - *Not only... but also:* *Not only did she complete the project early, but she also reduced costs.*
  - *Only when / Only after:* *Only after receiving the report did the manager realize the mistake.*

## PART 2 — PARTICIPLE CLAUSES, ADVANCED PASSIVES & SUBJUNCTIVE

### UNIT 4: Participle Clauses (Present, Past & Perfect Participles)
- **Present Participle Clauses (`Verb-ing`):** Replaces active clauses expressing simultaneous actions or reasons.
  - *Realizing that he was late, he took a taxi.* (= Because he realized that he was late...)
  - *Walking down the corridor, I ran into the CEO.* (= While I was walking down the corridor...)
- **Past Participle Clauses (`Verb-ed / V3`):** Replaces passive clauses.
  - *Built in 1850, the mansion requires extensive restoration.* (= Which was built in 1850...)
  - *Impressed by her credentials, the company offered her the executive role.*
- **Perfect Participle Clauses (`Having + Past Participle`):** Emphasizes that one action was completed BEFORE another.
  - *Having completed the audit, the accountant submitted the final report.* (= After he had completed the audit...)

### UNIT 5: Advanced Passive Structures & Impersonal Reporting
- **Passive Reporting Structures:**
  - `It + is/was + Passive Verb + THAT clause`
    - *It is reported that negotiations have stalled.*
    - *It was believed that the artifact was lost forever.*
  - `Subject + is/was + Passive Verb + TO INFINITIVE / PERFECT INFINITIVE`
    - *The company is expected to release its financial figures tomorrow.*
    - *The novel is said to have been inspired by real events.* (Perfect infinitive for past action)
- **Causative Passive (`Have / Get something done`):**
  - *We had the entire office renovated last month.*
  - *She gets her car serviced every six months.*

### UNIT 6: The Subjunctive Mood & Unreal Past
- **Subjunctive in Formal Clauses:** Used after verbs of demand, recommendation, or requirement (*demand, suggest, insist, recommend, require, it is essential that*). The verb remains in base form (`be, go, work`) regardless of subject.
  - *The director insisted that he be present at the meeting.* (Not *he is* or *he was*)
  - *It is essential that every candidate submit their portfolio on time.*
- **Unreal Past (`Wishes, Suppositions & Would Rather`):**
  - `I wish / If only + Past Simple` (Present regret): *I wish I had more free time.*
  - `I wish / If only + Past Perfect` (Past regret): *If only I had taken that opportunity!*
  - `I'd rather + Past Simple` (Preference about someone else): *I'd rather you didn't smoke here.*
