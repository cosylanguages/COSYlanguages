## Executive Overview & Architectural System

This master specification defines the comprehensive intermediate curriculum for the COSYlanguages B1 English ecosystem (unifying **B1.1** and **B1.2** CEFR sub-levels). At B1, learners expand fluency beyond basic survival to express complex personal opinions, argue points of view, handle workplace negotiations, write structured essays/emails, describe past experiences with precise narrative tenses, and navigate abstract themes in society, technology, media, and environmental sustainability.

## PART 1 — PERSONALITY, PSYCHOLOGY & EMOTIONS

### UNIT 1: Complex Character Traits, Identity & Relationships (B1.1)
- **1.1 Complex Personality Nuances & Identity**
  - **Core Vocabulary:** Identity, ambition, empathy, self-confidence, insecurity, temperament, charisma, reliability, integrity, attitude, perspective, self-esteem.
  - **Essential Verbs:** Express, project, reflect, admire, struggle with, overcome, perceive, misjudge.
  - **Adjectives (Nuanced Character):** Ambitious, compassionate, insecure, charismatic, level-headed, unpredictable, conscientious, arrogant, humble, introverted, extroverted, dependable, manipulative, sincere.
  - **Collocations:** Highly ambitious, level-headed leader, charismatic personality, quiet confidence, boost self-esteem, project confidence.
  - **Set Phrases:** She strikes me as very level-headed. / What makes him so charismatic? / He struggles with self-doubt despite his success. / First impressions can often be misleading.
  - **Sentence Frames:** `What I admire most about her is + [Quality]`, `He has a tendency to be + [Adjective] + under pressure`.
  - **Micro-Dialogue:**
    - *A:* How did the interview with the candidate go?
    - *B:* She made a great impression! She strikes me as highly conscientious and level-headed.
    - *A:* Excellent, that's exactly the kind of temperament we need for this role.

- **1.2 Interpersonal Relationships, Boundaries & Trust**
  - **Core Vocabulary:** Relationship dynamic, boundary, trust, betrayal, intimacy, mutual respect, compromise, obligation, expectation, loyalty.
  - **Essential Verbs:** Establish boundaries, compromise, betray, rebuild trust, resolve differences, maintain, value, cherish.
  - **Adjectives:** Trustworthy, supportive, toxic, distant, mutual, unconditional.
  - **Collocations:** Establish clear boundaries, mutual respect, build trust, breach of trust, reach a compromise, toxic relationship.
  - **Set Phrases:** Relationships require mutual respect and compromise. / It takes a long time to rebuild trust after a betrayal. / You need to set clear boundaries with your colleagues.
  - **Micro-Dialogue:**
    - *A:* I've been having some arguments with my roommate regarding chores.
    - *B:* Have you tried sitting down to establish clear boundaries?
    - *A:* Not yet. We need to reach a fair compromise that works for both of us.

## PART 1 — COMPLEX TENSES & NARRATIVE ASPECT (B1.1 & B1.2)

### UNIT 1: Present Perfect Continuous vs. Present Perfect Simple
- **Structural System:**
  - *Present Perfect Continuous:* `Subject + have/has been + Verb-ing` (*I have been working for three hours.*)
  - *Present Perfect Simple:* `Subject + have/has + Past Participle (V3)` (*I have written three emails.*)
- **Core Distinction:**
  - *Present Perfect Continuous* emphasizes the duration, process, or temporary nature of an ongoing action. (*She has been painting the kitchen all morning.*)
  - *Present Perfect Simple* emphasizes the completion, result, or quantity. (*She has painted two walls.*)
- **Action vs. Stative Verbs:** Stative verbs (know, like, believe, understand) are NOT used in the continuous form. (*I have known him for years* - NOT *I have been knowing him*).
- **Common Mistakes:**
  - ❌ *I have been knowing her for ten years.* ➔ ✔️ *I have known her for ten years.*
  - ❌ *He works here since 2015.* ➔ ✔️ *He has been working here since 2015.*
- **Mini Dialogue:**
  - *A:* You look tired! What have you been doing?
  - *B:* I have been running in the park for two hours. I've covered 15 kilometers!

### UNIT 2: Past Perfect Simple & Past Perfect Continuous (Narrative Mastery)
- **Structural System:**
  - *Past Perfect Simple:* `Subject + had + Past Participle (V3)`
  - *Past Perfect Continuous:* `Subject + had been + Verb-ing`
- **Core Rules & Usage:**
  - Used to describe an action that happened BEFORE another action in the past (the "past of the past").
  - *Past Perfect Simple:* Action completed before a point in the past. (*When we arrived at the station, the train had already left.*)
  - *Past Perfect Continuous:* Duration of an action continuing up to a point in the past. (*He was exhausted because he had been driving all night.*)
- **Examples:**
  - *She realized that she had forgotten her keys after she locked the door.*
  - *They had been negotiating for three hours when an agreement was finally reached.*
- **Common Mistakes:**
  - ❌ *When I arrived at the cinema, the film already started.* ➔ ✔️ *When I arrived at the cinema, the film had already started.*

### UNIT 3: Passive Voice Across Tenses
- **Structural System:** `Subject + Form of BE + Past Participle (V3) (+ by Agent)`
- **Tense Transformations:**
  - *Present Simple Passive:* `am/is/are + V3` (*The reports are reviewed daily.*)
  - *Past Simple Passive:* `was/were + V3` (*The bridge was built in 1920.*)
  - *Present Continuous Passive:* `am/is/are being + V3` (*The house is being painted right now.*)
  - *Present Perfect Passive:* `have/has been + V3` (*The contract has been signed.*)
  - *Future Passive (Will):* `will be + V3` (*The decision will be announced tomorrow.*)
- **Usage:** Used when the action itself or the receiver of the action is more important than the agent, or when the agent is unknown.

## PART 2 — CONDITIONALS & MODALS OF DEDUCTION

### UNIT 4: Second & Third Conditionals
- **Second Conditional (Unreal Present/Future):**
  - `IF + Past Simple, WOULD / COULD + Base Verb`
  - *If I had more time, I would take up photography.*
- **Third Conditional (Unreal Past / Regrets):**
  - `IF + Past Perfect, WOULD HAVE / COULD HAVE + Past Participle (V3)`
  - Usage: Imagining alternative outcomes for past events.
  - *If I had studied harder, I would have passed the exam.*
  - *If they had left earlier, they wouldn't have missed their flight.*
- **Common Mistakes:**
  - ❌ *If I would have known, I would have called you.* ➔ ✔️ *If I had known, I would have called you.*

### UNIT 5: Modals of Deduction & Speculation (Present & Past)
- **Present Speculation (`Modal + Base Verb`):**
  - *Must be* (95% certainty positive): *He has three luxury cars; he must be wealthy.*
  - *Can't be* (95% certainty negative): *She just ate a huge meal; she can't be hungry.*
  - *Might / May / Could be* (50% possibility): *He isn't answering his phone; he might be in a meeting.*
- **Past Speculation (`Modal + HAVE + Past Participle`):**
  - *Must have + V3:* *The ground is wet; it must have rained last night.*
  - *Can't / Couldn't have + V3:* *He couldn't have stolen the money; he was with me all evening.*
  - *Might / May / Could have + V3:* *She missed the bus; she might have slept in.*

## PART 3 — REPORTED SPEECH & VERB PATTERNS

### UNIT 6: Advanced Reported Speech & Reporting Verbs
- **Reporting Questions:**
  - *Wh- Questions:* `He asked me where I lived.` (Word order changes to statement: subject + verb).
  - *Yes/No Questions:* `She asked if / whether I was coming to the party.`
- **Reporting Orders & Requests:** `Verb + Object + to / not to + Infinitive`
  - *The doctor advised me to rest.* / *She asked him not to smoke.*
- **Specialized Reporting Verbs:**
  - *Offer to do:* `He offered to help.`
  - *Refuse to do:* `She refused to sign.`
  - *Apologize for doing:* `He apologized for being late.`
  - *Suggest doing:* `She suggested eating at the Italian restaurant.`

### UNIT 7: Verb Patterns (Gerunds vs. Infinitives with Meaning Change)
- **Verb + Infinitive (`to + V`):** *decide, hope, agree, promise, refuse, manage, afford*.
- **Verb + Gerund (`V-ing`):** *enjoy, avoid, consider, mind, suggest, practice, risk*.
- **Verbs Changing Meaning with Gerund/Infinitive:**
  - *Remember / Forget:*
    - `Remember to do` (future duty): *Remember to lock the door!*
    - `Remember doing` (past memory): *I remember locking the door before leaving.*
  - *Stop:*
    - `Stop to do` (pause to do something else): *He stopped to buy a coffee.*
    - `Stop doing` (quit an activity): *He stopped smoking last year.*
  - *Try:*
    - `Try to do` (make an effort): *I tried to lift the heavy box.*
    - `Try doing` (experiment): *If you have a headache, try drinking water.*
