# 🎙️ COSY Blog & Magazine — English Voice Audio Recording Scripts

This document contains page-by-page audio recording scripts for all English blog post flipbook pages.
Each script is adapted for the target CEFR level to assist learners with listening practice and accessibility.

## 🎧 Recording Guidelines & Level Directions

### CEFR A0–A1 / A2 (Beginner Level)
- **Target Post:** `stop-translating-10-sentence-frames.md`
- **Pacing:** Moderate, clear, and unhurried (approx. 130–140 words per minute).
- **Tone:** Encouraging, friendly, warm, and highly articulate.
- **Pauses:** Distinct 1-second pause after each sentence frame and bullet example. Emphasize key structural words (*who*, *where*, *thing that*, *use to*).

### CEFR B1–B2 (Intermediate Level)
- **Target Posts:** `replace-50-overused-phrases.md`, `replace-very-50-stronger-adjectives.md`, `top-10-verb-synonyms-intermediate-plateau.md`
- **Pacing:** Natural, conversational, and expressive (approx. 150–160 words per minute).
- **Tone:** Engaging editorial tone, like an upbeat podcast host or teacher.
- **Pauses:** Natural pauses before contrasts (*Instead of saying X... try using Y!*).

## 📜 1. Stop Translating! 10 Reusable Sentence Frames (CEFR A0–B1)
**Audio Folder Target:** `audio/blog/stop-translating-10-sentence-frames-page-{N}.mp3`

### Page 1: Introduction to Circumlocution
**Vocal Direction:** *Warm, reassuring voice. Pause slightly before key bold terms.*
> "Welcome to Page 1 of 'Stop Translating! 10 Reusable Sentence Frames That Let You Speak From Day One'.
> One of the biggest obstacles for beginner and intermediate language learners is the habit of translating word by word. When you don't know an exact word—like corkscrew, pharmacy, or architect—your brain often freezes.
> Our methodology praises eliminating translation in favor of a creative, natural approach. Native speakers don't know every single word in the dictionary either; what they have is the ability to describe what they don't know. Circumlocution is a real communication skill!
> Master these ten reusable sentence frames and twenty survival phrases. Each frame acts like a LEGO piece: combine it with simple words to create hundreds of original sentences."

### Page 2: The Core Shift
**Vocal Direction:** *Clear tabular reading. Contrast the left column with the right column clearly.*
> "Welcome to Page 2: The Core Shift from Translation to Description.
> Here is how you shift your thinking:
> Instead of searching for basic identification... say: 'It's a...'
> Instead of searching for professions... say: 'It's a person who...'
> Instead of searching for locations... say: 'It's a place where...'
> Instead of searching for objects... say: 'It's a thing that...'
> Instead of searching for functions... say: 'It's something you use to...'
> Instead of searching for moments... say: 'It's a time when...'
> Instead of searching for dates... say: 'It's a day when...'
> To give examples, say: 'For example...'
> To explain definitions, say: 'It means...'
> And to ask for help in English, ask: 'How do you say this in English?'"

### Page 3: The 10 Essential Sentence Frames
**Vocal Direction:** *Read each numbered frame distinctly, followed by example sentences.*
> "Welcome to Page 3: The 10 Essential Sentence Frames.
> Frame 1: It's a... Examples: It's a book. It's a problem. It's a restaurant.
> Frame 2: It's a person who... Examples: It's a person who teaches children—a teacher. It's a person who helps sick people—a doctor.
> Frame 3: It's a place where... Examples: It's a place where people buy food—a market. It's a place where you sleep—a hotel room.
> Frame 4: It's a thing that... Examples: It's a thing that tells the time—a clock. It's a thing that opens doors—a key.
> Frame 5: It's something you use to... Examples: It's something you use to cut bread—a knife. It's something you use to write—a pen.
> Frame 6: It's a time when... Examples: It's a time when people sleep—nighttime.
> Frame 7: It's a day when... Examples: It's a day when families meet—a holiday.
> Frame 8: For example... Example: I like sports. For example, football and tennis.
> Frame 9: It means... Example: 'Huge' means very big.
> Frame 10: How do you say... in English?"

### Page 4: Sensory & Descriptive Anchors
**Vocal Direction:** *Dynamic, descriptive tone.*
> "Welcome to Page 4: Sensory & Descriptive Anchors.
> When an object is hard to define, anchor it with sensory descriptions:
> 'It's like...' -> It's like a big dog.
> 'It's for...' -> It's for cooking.
> 'You use it to...' -> You use it to open bottles.
> 'It looks like...' -> It looks like a bird.
> 'It sounds...' -> It sounds interesting.
> 'It feels...' -> It feels cold.
> 'It has...' -> It has four wheels.
> 'It comes from...' -> It comes from Italy.
> 'It's made of...' -> It's made of wood."

### Page 5: 20 Survival Phrases
**Vocal Direction:** *Brisk, clear rhythm.*
> "Welcome to Page 5: 20 Survival Phrases When You Forget a Word.
> Bookmark these phrases so you never get stuck:
> One: It's a kind of... Two: It's a thing that... Three: It's something that... Four: It's a person who... Five: It's a place where...
> Six: It's a time when... Seven: It's like... Eight: It looks like... Nine: It sounds like... Ten: It has...
> Eleven: You use it to... Twelve: You can use it for... Thirteen: It is used for... Fourteen: It is made of... Fifteen: It is similar to...
> Sixteen: It is the opposite of... Seventeen: It means... Eighteen: It is the same as... Nineteen: I don't know the word, but... Twenty: How do you say...?"

### Page 6: Multilingual Summary
**Vocal Direction:** *Clear pronunciation of foreign language phrases in French, Russian, Italian, and Greek.*
> "Welcome to Page 6: Multilingual Summary for International Learners.
> Sentence frames work in every language!
> In French: C'est une personne qui... C'est un endroit où... C'est quelque chose qu'on utilise pour...
> In Russian: Это человек, который... Это место, где... Это вещь, которую используют для...
> In Italian: È una persona che... È un posto dove... È qualcosa che si usa per...
> In Greek: Είναι ένα άτομο που... Είναι ένα μέρος όπου..."

### Page 7: Conclusion
**Vocal Direction:** *Inspirational, closing statement.*
> "Welcome to Page 7: Conclusion.
> Remember: Fluency is not about knowing every single word—it's about never staying silent! Whenever you forget a word, grab one of these sentence frames, describe what you mean, and keep speaking!"

## 📜 2. Stop Saying 'Very'! 50 Stronger Words (CEFR B1–B2)
**Audio Folder Target:** `audio/blog/replace-very-50-stronger-adjectives-page-{N}.mp3`

### Page 1: The Intermediate Adjective Trap
> "Welcome to Page 1 of Stop Saying 'Very'! 50 Stronger Words Every Intermediate English Learner Should Know.
> Reaching the intermediate plateau often means relying on 'very' to intensify adjectives. Saying 'very big' or 'very good' is correct, but repeating 'very' makes your speech monotonous. Upgrading to single-word adjectives makes your English sound natural, sophisticated, and vivid."

### Page 2: Better Alternatives (Part 1)
> "Welcome to Page 2: Adjective Upgrades 1 to 25.
> Instead of Very bad -> say Awful, Terrible, Dreadful.
> Instead of Very good -> say Excellent, Outstanding, Superb.
> Instead of Very big -> say Enormous, Massive.
> Instead of Very small -> say Tiny, Miniature.
> Instead of Very beautiful -> say Gorgeous, Breathtaking.
> Instead of Very interesting -> say Fascinating, Captivating.
> Instead of Very funny -> say Hilarious.
> Instead of Very tired -> say Exhausted, Drained.
> Instead of Very cold -> say Freezing.
> Instead of Very hot -> say Boiling, Scorching."

### Page 3: Better Alternatives (Part 2)
> "Welcome to Page 3: Adjective Upgrades 26 to 50.
> Instead of Very clean -> say Spotless, Immaculate.
> Instead of Very dirty -> say Filthy.
> Instead of Very important -> say Crucial, Essential, Vital.
> Instead of Very afraid -> say Terrified, Petrified.
> Instead of Very smart -> say Brilliant.
> Instead of Very busy -> say Hectic, Swamped.
> Practice substituting these words into your daily journal today!"
