For many intermediate language learners, grammar lessons feel like endless drills: subject plus verb plus object, rigid tense charts, and strict exception lists. But when you step beyond textbook pages into authentic conversations, podcasts, and modern media, grammar reveals itself as a vibrant, living ecosystem.

In this October Autumn Special Edition of COSYmagazine, we examine my Top 5 Favourite English Grammar Rules through a Vogue Gazette editorial lens — combining CELTA-aligned pedagogical CCQs with playful, contemporary spoken structures used by native speakers every day.

1. The Softening Power of the -ish Suffix 🍂

1. Meaning: Core Definition & Nationalities

Core Meaning: The suffix -ish traditionally forms adjectives of nationality, origin, or language membership.

  • Standard Nationalities & Languages:
    • Spanish — "She speaks fluent Spanish and loves Spanish music."
    • English — "English grammar offers creative conversational patterns."
    • Danish — "Danish furniture is celebrated for minimalist design."
    • Finnish — "Finnish education emphasizes play and pupil well-being."

2. Special Usage: Time, Age, Behaviour & Modifiers

In modern spoken English, -ish functions as a delicate conversational softener:

  • a) Time & Age (Approximation):
    • Formal: "Let us meet at exactly 7:00 PM."
    • Informal / Natural: "Let's meet at seven-ish." (Around 7:00)
    • Formal: "The applicant appears to be thirty years of age."
    • Informal / Natural: "He looks thirty-ish."
  • b) Behaviour & Personality:
    • Well-Known Adjectives: childish (immature), stylish (fashionable), foolish (unwise).
    • Creative Modern Coinages: babyish, introvert-ish, adult-ish.
    • Formal: "His reaction was somewhat immature." vs. Informal: "He was being a bit babyish."
  • c) Prepositions & Modifiers:
    • Formal: "The venue is located in close proximity to the central station."
    • Informal / Natural: "It's near-ish to the station."
    • Formal: "I feel slightly hungry." vs. Informal: "I'm hungry-ish!"

3. Synonyms & Related Suffixes (-like, -esque)

Expand your stylistic range with two complementary suffixes:

  • -like (Resemblance / Character):
    • Examples: adult-like, interim baby-like, cat-like.
    • Formal: "He demonstrated remarkable maturity." vs. Informal: "He handled the issue in an adult-like way."
  • -esque (Artistic & Atmospheric Flair):
    • Meaning: Resembling the artistic style, aesthetic, or spirit of a icon or era.
    • Examples: van-Gogh-esque, Shakespeare-esque, Kafka-esque.
    • Spoken Example: "The autumn leaves yesterday gave off a wonderfully van-Gogh-esque landscape."

4. Wrap-Up & CELTA CCQ Summary

CELTA CCQs: Does "seven-ish" mean exactly 7:00? (No, approximately around 7:00). Is "van-Gogh-esque" literal or stylistic? (Stylistic resemblance).

Summary: Use -ish for casual softening, -like for behavioral resemblance, and -esque for artistic flair!

2. Emotional -ing with Stative Verbs ☕

1. Meaning: Stative vs Dynamic Shift

Standard textbook rules state that stative verbs (love, hate, like, understand, want) describe permanent mental states and cannot take the -ing continuous form. In spoken English, shifting stative verbs into continuous forms signals real-time emotion.

2. Special Usage: Real-Time Emotions & Social Check-ins

  • a) Present Emotion: "I'm loving this hot autumn cider!" (Heightened current joy)
  • b) Temporary Dislike: "I'm hating this rain today." (Immediate reaction)
  • c) Social Check-in: "Are you liking the workshop?" (Asking about live experience)

3. Synonyms & Expressive Upgrades

  • Formal State: "I thoroughly enjoy this topic."
  • Spoken Upgrade: "I'm really feeling this topic today!" / "I'm loving this!"

4. Wrap-Up & CELTA CCQ Summary

CELTA CCQs: Does "I'm loving this" mean a permanent personality trait or a live emotional experience? (An immediate live emotional experience right now).

Summary: Use the -ing continuous form on stative verbs when you want your voice to sound warm, energetic, and emotionally present!

3. Modern Dynamic Action Verbing: "Coffeeing" & "Machining" ☕⚙️

1. Meaning: Turning Any Noun into an Active State

Beyond traditional dictionary definitions, modern spoken English employs an ultra-productive pattern: taking any noun or machine and turning it into an active continuous verb ([Noun] is [Noun]-ing) to express whether an object, person, or brain is functioning as expected.

2. Special Usage: "Coffeeing", "Machining" & "Braining"

Compare the contrast between full functionality and total breakdown across everyday objects and states:

  • a) Work & Effort:
    • "Work is working!" — My job routine or strategy is producing great results.
    • "Work is not working!" — My current system or workflow is failing.
  • b) Coffee & Energy:
    • "Coffee is coffee-ing today!" — The morning espresso has kicked in and given me great focus.
    • "Coffee is not coffee-ing..." — I drank two cups, but I am still exhausted.
  • c) Technology, Music & Mind:
    • "The machine is not machining." — The printer or laptop is glitching and refuses to cooperate.
    • "This music is music-ing!" — The playlist hits the exact right mood for this evening.
    • "My brain is not braining today." — I am too tired to solve complex problems right now.

3. Synonyms & Formal vs Spoken Comparison

Formal / Textbook Equivalent Modern Spoken COSY Upgrade
"The coffee is having a stimulating effect." "The coffee is coffee-ing!"
"The equipment is experiencing technical difficulties." "The machine is not machining."
"I am suffering from mental fatigue." "My brain is not braining today."

4. Wrap-Up & CELTA CCQ Summary

CELTA CCQs: If someone says "My brain is not braining", is their brain broken? (No, they are humorously expressing mental fatigue). Is this used in formal academic papers? (No, strictly in casual, expressive spoken contexts).

Summary: Turn any noun into an active verb with -ing to express whether a tool, beverage, or state is fulfilling its purpose!

4. Verb Shifting (Turning Nouns into Active Verbs) 💻

1. Meaning: Zero-Derivation ("Verbing")

English routinely converts nouns directly into active verbs without changing forms, creating vivid, concise spoken shortcuts.

2. Special Usage: Social Habits & Modern Work

  • Social Habits: "Let's coffee tomorrow morning." (Go drink coffee together)
  • Work Routine: "We're desk-lunching all week." (Eating lunch while working at one's desk)
  • Modern Responsibilities: "I am adulting today by organizing my tax documents."

3. Synonyms & Phrasal Equivalents

  • Formal Phrase: "Search for information using Google."
  • Verb Shift Upgrade: "Just Google it."

4. Wrap-Up & CELTA CCQ Summary

CELTA CCQs: When you say "Let me bookmark this", did you physically insert a paper ribbon? (No, saved a digital link). Does verb shifting make spoken English longer or shorter? (Shorter and more direct).

Summary: Turn familiar nouns into active verbs to make your English sound direct, modern, and effortless.

5. Modern Vibe Evaluators: "It's giving..." 🎃

1. Meaning: Aesthetic & Mood Evaluation

The popular spoken evaluation frame "It's giving..." functions as shorthand for "It radiates the aura, energy, or aesthetic of...".

2. Special Usage: Aesthetic, Atmosphere & Vibe

  • Atmosphere: "This autumn cafe is giving cozy October vibes."
  • Style & Presence: "It's giving main character energy."
  • Quality & Impression: "That trench coat is giving luxury."

3. Synonyms & Traditional Alternatives

  • Formal: "It creates an atmosphere reminiscent of..."
  • Spoken COSY Upgrade: "It's giving..."

4. Wrap-Up & CELTA CCQ Summary

CELTA CCQs: Does "It's giving luxury" mean the object is literally giving you money? (No, it looks and feels high-end). Is it used to describe physical objects or general moods? (Both).

Summary: Use "It's giving..." to evaluate aesthetic, mood, and atmosphere naturally during spoken discussions.

Summary Practice: Combining All 5 Rules 🍁

Before vs After Conversational Upgrade

Rigid Textbook B1 Version:
"Let us meet around seven o'clock. I am enjoying this coffee shop very much. My coffee is having a stimulating effect today. We are eating lunch at our desks now. This cafe decor looks like a luxury resort."

Natural Expressive B2 Vogue Gazette Version:
"Let's meet at seven-ish if that works for you! I'm loving this autumn spot — the coffee is definitely coffee-ing today! While we're desk-lunching, notice how this whole setup is giving cozy luxury."

Mastering grammar isn't about avoiding mistakes: it is about choosing the right linguistic tools to make your voice memorable, natural, and uniquely yours!
