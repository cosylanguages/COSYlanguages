For many language learners, grammar lessons feel like endless drills: subject plus verb plus object, rigid tense charts, and strict exception lists. But when you step beyond textbook pages into authentic conversations, podcasts, and modern media, grammar reveals itself as a vibrant, living ecosystem.

In this October Autumn Special Edition of COSYmagazine, we examine my Top 5 Favourite English Grammar Rules through an editorial Vogue Gazette lens — combining CELTA-aligned pedagogical CCQs with playful, contemporary spoken structures used by native speakers every day.

1. The Softening Power of the -ish Suffix 🍂

1. Meaning: Core Definition & Nationalities

Core Meaning: The suffix -ish traditionally forms adjectives indicating national origin, belonging, or language membership.

  • Standard Nationalities & Languages:
    • Spanish — "She speaks fluent Spanish and loves Spanish music."
    • English — "English grammar offers creative conversational patterns."
    • Danish — "Danish furniture is celebrated for minimalist design."
    • Finnish — baseline "Finnish education emphasizes play and pupil well-being."

2. Special Usage: Time, Age, Behaviour & Modifiers

In modern spoken English, -ish functions as a versatile conversational softener:

  • a) Time & Age (Approximation):
    • Formal: "Let us meet at exactly 7:00 PM."
    • Informal / Natural: "Let's meet at seven-ish." (Around 7:00)
    • Formal: "The applicant appears to be thirty years of age."
    • Informal / Natural: "He looks thirty-ish."
  • b) Behaviour & Personality:
    • Well-Known Adjectives: childish (immature), stylish (fashionable), foolish (unwise).
    • Creative Modern Coinages: babyish, introvert-ish, adult-ish.
    • Formal: "His reaction was somewhat immature." vs. Informal: "He was being a bit babyish."
  • c) Prepositions & Modifiers:
    • Formal: "The venue is located in close proximity to the central station."
    • Informal / Natural: "It's near-ish to the station."
    • Formal: "I feel slightly hungry." vs. Informal: "I'm hungry-ish!"

3. Synonyms & Related Suffixes (-like, -esque)

Expand your stylistic range with two complementary suffixes:

  • -like (Resemblance / Character):
    • Examples: adult-like, baby-like, cat-like.
    • Formal: "He demonstrated remarkable maturity." vs. Informal: "He handled the issue in an adult-like way."
  • -esque (Artistic & Atmospheric Flair):
    • Meaning: Resembling the artistic style, aesthetic, or spirit of a famous figure or era.
    • Examples: van-Gogh-esque, Shakespeare-esque, Kafka-esque.
    • Spoken Example: "The autumn leaves yesterday gave off a wonderfully van-Gogh-esque landscape."

4. Wrap-Up & CELTA CCQ Summary

CELTA CCQs: Does "seven-ish" mean exactly 7:00? (No, approximately around 7:00). Is "van-Gogh-esque" literal or stylistic? (Stylistic resemblance).

Formal Exam Tip (IELTS/TOEFL): In formal academic writing, replace -ish with formal hedging modifiers: approximately, roughly, or moderately.

2. Emotional -ing with Stative Verbs ☕

1. Meaning: Stative vs Dynamic Emotion Shift

Traditional textbooks teach that stative verbs (love, hate, like, understand, want) describe permanent mental states and cannot take the -ing continuous form. In live spoken English, shifting stative verbs into continuous forms conveys heightened, real-time emotion.

2. Special Usage: Real-Time Emotions & Social Check-ins

  • Present Emotion: "I'm loving this hot autumn cider!" (Heightened current joy)
  • Temporary Dislike: "I'm hating this rain today." (Immediate reaction)
  • Social Check-in: "Are you liking the workshop?" (Asking about live experience)

3. Formal vs Spoken Register Comparison

Formal Academic / Writing Exam Register 📝 Spoken COSY Manner Upgrade 🗣️
"I thoroughly enjoy this topic." "I'm really loving this topic today!"
"Do you find the event agreeable?" "Are you liking the event?"

4. Wrap-Up & CELTA CCQ Summary

CELTA CCQs: Does "I'm loving this coffee" mean a permanent state or an immediate experience? (An immediate live emotional experience right now).

3. Modern Dynamic Action Verbing: "Coffeeing" & "Machining" ☕⚙️

1. Meaning: Turning Nouns into Active Functional Verbs

Modern spoken English uses an ultra-productive pattern: turning any noun or machine into an active continuous verb ([Noun] is [Noun]-ing) to express whether an object, person, or brain is fulfilling its purpose.

2. Special Usage: "Coffeeing", "Machining" & "Braining"

  • Work & Effort: "Work is working!" (Great results) vs "Work is not working!" (Workflow failure).
  • Coffee & Energy: "Coffee is coffee-ing today!" (Great focus) vs "Coffee is not coffee-ing..." (Still tired).
  • Technology & Mind: "The machine is not machining today." / "My brain is not braining."

3. Formal vs Spoken Register Comparison

Formal / Exam Register 📝 Spoken COSY Manner Upgrade 🗣️
"The coffee is producing a stimulating effect." "The coffee is coffee-ing!"
"The equipment is experiencing technical failure." "The machine is not machining."
"I am suffering from mental fatigue." "My brain is not braining today."

4. Wrap-Up & CELTA CCQ Summary

CELTA CCQs: When someone says "My brain is not braining", is it literal or humorous? (Humorous admission of tiredness).

4. Zero-Derivation Verb Shifting (Nouns into Active Verbs) 💻

1. Meaning: Zero-Derivation ("Verbing")

English converts well-known nouns directly into verbs without changing forms, creating concise, high-frequency active verbs.

2. Special Usage: Social Habits & Modern Work

  • Social Habits: "Let's coffee tomorrow morning." (Go drink coffee together)
  • Work Routine: "We're desk-lunching all week." (Eating lunch at one's desk)
  • Modern Responsibilities: "I am adulting today by organizing my tax documents."

3. Formal Academic Verbs for Language Exams 📝

High-scoring C1 academic verbs derived directly from nouns:

  • to benchmark — "The report benchmarks current environmental standards."
  • to spearhead — "The organization spearheaded a new initiative."
  • to bottleneck — "Supply chain issues bottlenecked production."

4. Wrap-Up & CELTA CCQ Summary

CELTA CCQs: Does verb shifting change the noun spelling? (No). Are verbs like to benchmark accepted in formal C1 essays? (Yes).

5. Modern Vibe Evaluators: "It's giving..." 🎃

1. Meaning: Aesthetic & Mood Evaluation

The popular spoken frame "It's giving..." functions as shorthand for "It radiates the aura, energy, or aesthetic of...".

2. Special Usage: Aesthetic & Atmosphere

  • Atmosphere: "This autumn cafe is giving cozy October vibes."
  • Style & Presence: "It's giving main character energy."
  • Quality & Impression: "That trench coat is giving luxury."

3. Formal vs Spoken Comparison

Exam Formal Register (IELTS / Cambridge Essays) 📝 Spoken COSY Manner Upgrade 🗣️
"The architecture is evocative of nineteenth-century design." "It's giving nineteenth-century vibes."
"The atmosphere exudes tranquility and elegance." "It's giving luxury and peace."

4. Wrap-Up & CELTA CCQ Summary

CELTA CCQs: Does "It's giving luxury" mean someone gives you money? (No, it looks high-end). What formal phrase replaces it in IELTS essays? ("It is evocative of...").

Summary Practice: Combining All 5 Rules 🍁

Before vs After Conversational Upgrade

Rigid Textbook B1 Version:
"Let us meet around seven o'clock. I am enjoying this coffee shop very much. My coffee is having a stimulating effect today. We are eating lunch at our desks now. This cafe decor looks like a luxury resort."

Natural Expressive B2 Vogue Gazette Version:
"Let's meet at seven-ish if that works for you! I'm loving this autumn spot — the coffee is definitely coffee-ing today! While we're desk-lunching, notice how this whole setup is giving cozy luxury."

Mastering grammar isn't about avoiding mistakes: it is about choosing the right linguistic tools to make your voice memorable, natural, and uniquely yours across writing exams and everyday life!
