My Top 5 Favourite English Grammar Rules for Intermediate Learners

🎯 CELTA Pedagogical Focus

🎙️ Podcast Host Prompts

For many language learners, grammar feels like a strict set of rules designed to catch mistakes. Traditional textbooks often focus on rigid formulas: subject plus verb plus object, strict tense charts, and endless lists of exceptions.

However, when you reach the intermediate level (CEFR B1–B2), grammar transforms into something much more exciting: a creative toolkit for expressing emotion, playfulness, and social connection. Native speakers constantly adapt and stretch grammar patterns to communicate subtle shades of meaning.

Here are my top 5 favourite grammar rules in English that go beyond standard textbook formulas and give your intermediate English immediate natural flair.

1. The Softening Power of the -ish Suffix

1. Meaning: Core Definition & Nationalities

Core Meaning: The suffix -ish traditionally signifies origin, language, or belonging to a nation or group.

  • Standard Nationalities & Languages:
    • Spanish — "She speaks fluent Spanish and loves Spanish music."
    • English — "English grammar offers creative conversational patterns."
    • Danish — "Danish furniture is celebrated for minimalist design."
    • Finnish — "Finnish education emphasizes play and pupil well-being."

2. Special Usage: Time, Age, Behaviour & Modifiers

In spoken English, -ish serves as a versatile conversational softener:

  • a) Time & Age (Approximation):
    • Formal: "Let us meet at exactly 7:00 PM."
    • Informal / Natural: "Let's meet at seven-ish." (Around 7:00)
    • Formal: "The applicant appears to be thirty years old."
    • Informal / Natural: "He looks thirty-ish."
  • b) Behaviour & Personality:
    • Well-Known Adjectives: childish (immature), stylish (fashionable), foolish (unwise).
    • Creative Modern Coinages: babyish, introvert-ish, adult-ish.
    • Formal: "His reaction was somewhat immature." vs. Informal: "He was being babyish."
  • c) Prepositions & Modifiers:
    • Formal: "The venue is located near the central station."
    • Informal / Natural: "It's near-ish to the station."
    • Formal: "I feel slightly hungry." vs. Informal: "I'm hungry-ish!"

3. Synonyms & Related Suffixes (-like, -esque)

Expand your expressive range with two complementary suffixes:

  • -like (Resemblance / Character):
    • Examples: adult-like, baby-like, cat-like.
    • Formal: "He showed great composure." vs. Informal: "He reacted in an adult-like manner."
  • -esque (Artistic & Stylistic Atmosphere):
    • Meaning: Resembling the artistic style, aesthetic, or spirit of a famous figure or era.
    • Examples: van-Gogh-esque, Shakespeare-esque, Kafka-esque.
    • Spoken Example: "The sunset yesterday had a dramatic van-Gogh-esque sky."

4. Wrap-Up & Summary

Summary: Use -ish for casual softening, -like for physical/behavioral resemblance, and -esque for artistic or dramatic flair!

2. Emotional -ing with Stative Verbs

1. Meaning: Stative vs Dynamic Shift

Standard grammar states stative verbs (love, hate, like, understand, want) describe permanent states and cannot take the -ing continuous form. In spoken English, shifting stative verbs into continuous forms signals real-time emotion.

2. Special Usage: Real-Time Emotions & Social Check-ins

  • a) Present Emotion: "I'm loving this new podcast!" (Heightened current joy)
  • b) Temporary Dislike: "I'm hating this rainy weather today." (Immediate reaction)
  • c) Social Check-in: "Are you liking the conference?" (Asking about live experience)

3. Synonyms & Expressive Upgrades

  • Formal State: "I thoroughly enjoy this topic."
  • Spoken Upgrade: "I'm really feeling this topic today!" / "I'm loving this!"

4. Wrap-Up & Summary

Summary: Use the -ing form on stative verbs when you want your voice to sound warm, energetic, and emotionally present!

3. The Functional Multi-Tool: "Work is Working"

1. Meaning: The 4 Layers of "Work"

Beyond "having a job", the verb work covers functioning, effectiveness, and convenience:

  • Functioning: "The wifi is working."
  • Effective: "Our strategy is working!"
  • Convenient: "Does Tuesday work for you?"

2. Special Usage: "Work is Working" vs "Work is Not Working"

  • "Work is working!": My project or job is yielding fantastic results.
  • "Work is not working!": My current job setup or routine is causing frustration.

3. Synonyms & Expressive Alternatives

  • Formal: "Is this proposal suitable?" vs. Spoken: "Does this work?"
  • Formal: "The machinery is operational." vs. Spoken: "It's working."

4. Wrap-Up & Summary

Summary: Master the 4 layers of work to navigate workplace, scheduling, and technical conversations fluently.

4. Verb Shifting (Turning Nouns into Active Verbs)

1. Meaning: Zero-Derivation ("Verbing")

English routinely transforms well-known nouns directly into verbs without changing forms, creating economical spoken shortcuts.

2. Special Usage: Social Habits & Modern Work

  • Social: "Let's coffee tomorrow." (Go drink coffee together)
  • Work Routine: "We're desk-lunching today." (Eating lunch at work desk)
  • Modern Habits: "I'm adulting today by paying my taxes."

3. Synonyms & Phrasal Equivalents

  • Formal Phrase: "Search for information on Google."
  • Verb Shift Upgrade: "Just Google it."

4. Wrap-Up & Summary

Summary: Turn familiar nouns into verbs to make your English sound natural, direct, and contemporary.

5. Modern Vibe Evaluators: "It's giving..."

1. Meaning: Aesthetic & Mood Evaluation

The spoken frame "It's giving..." translates to "It radiates the vibe or aura of...".

2. Special Usage: Aesthetic, Atmosphere & Vibe

  • Atmosphere: "This cafe is giving 90s nostalgia."
  • Style & Confidence: "It's giving main character energy."
  • Quality & Impression: "That blazer is giving luxury."

3. Synonyms & Traditional Alternatives

  • Formal: "It creates an atmosphere reminiscent of..."
  • Spoken Upgrade: "It's giving..."

4. Wrap-Up & Summary

Summary: Use "It's giving..." to comment on atmosphere, aesthetic, and mood effortlessly during casual conversations.

Summary Practice: Combining All 5 Rules

Before vs After Comparison

Rigid Textbook B1 Version:
"Let us meet around seven o'clock. I am enjoying this restaurant very much. My new business plan is producing good results. We are eating lunch at our desks now. This decor looks like a luxury hotel."

Natural Expressive B2 Version:
"Let's meet at seven-ish if that works for you! I'm loving this new spot. The new strategy is definitely working, and while we're desk-lunching today, notice how this place is giving luxury resort."

Mastering grammar isn't about avoiding mistakes: it is about choosing the right linguistic tool to make your voice memorable!
