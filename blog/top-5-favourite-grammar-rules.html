1. The Softening Power of the -ish Suffix & Related Suffixes 🍂

1. Core Meaning & Nationalities (General Purpose Track 📖)

Core Meaning: The suffix -ish traditionally forms adjectives indicating national origin, belonging, or language membership.

  • Standard Nationalities:
    • Spanish — "She speaks fluent Spanish and loves Spanish literature."
    • English — "English grammar offers creative conversational flexibility."
    • Danish — "Danish interior design is celebrated for clean minimalism."
    • Finnish — "Finnish education emphasizes play and student well-being."

2. Special Usage Across Spoken, Relocation & Travel Tracks

In casual spoken English, -ish acts as a versatile conversational softening device:

  • a) Time & Age (Spoken 🗣️ & Travel ✈️ Tracks):
    • "Let me meet you at seven-ish near the central terminal." (Around 7:00)
    • "The tour guide looked thirty-ish." (Approximately thirty years old)
  • b) Behaviour & Personality (General 📖 & Spoken 🗣️ Tracks):
    • Well-Known Adjectives: childish (immature), stylish (fashionable), foolish (unwise).
    • Creative Modern Coinages: babyish, introvert-ish, adult-ish.
    • "He was feeling a bit introvert-ish today, so he stayed home."
  • c) Modifiers & Neighborhoods (Relocation Track 🏡):
    • "Our new apartment is in a quiet-ish suburban neighborhood."
    • "The local grocery store is open-ish until 9:00 PM."

3. Synonyms & Language Proficiency Exams Strategy 📝

Exam Prep Strategy (IELTS / TOEFL / Cambridge C1–C2): In formal academic writing, -ish is too informal. Exam essays require academic hedging modifiers:

Exam Formal Hedging (IELTS/TOEFL) 📝 Spoken COSY Manner Upgrade 🗣️
"The data exhibits approximately thirty percent growth." "It grew thirty-ish percent."
"The venue is located in close proximity to transit." "It's near-ish to the station."
"The policy yielded moderately positive outcomes." "It was good-ish."

Bonus Suffix Alternatives:

  • -like (Resemblance / Character): adult-like, baby-like, cat-like (Formal: "demonstrated adult-like composure").
  • -esque (Artistic & Atmospheric Flair): van-Gogh-esque, Shakespeare-esque, Kafka-esque (Formal: "reminiscent of van Gogh's artistic era").

4. Wrap-Up & CELTA CCQ Summary

CELTA CCQs: Is "seven-ish" acceptable in an IELTS Academic Task 2 essay? (No, use "approximately 7:00 PM"). Does "-ish" signal certainty or approximation? (Approximation and softening).

2. Emotional -ing with Stative Verbs ☕

1. Meaning: Stative vs Dynamic Emotion Shift

Traditional textbooks teach that stative verbs (love, hate, like, understand, want) describe permanent mental states and cannot take the -ing continuous form. In live spoken English, shifting stative verbs into continuous forms conveys heightened, real-time emotion.

2. Course Track Applications: Professional, Relocation & Travel

  • Professional Track 💼: "We're really loving the creative direction of this new brand campaign!" (Expressing live team enthusiasm during a client presentation).
  • Relocation Track 🏡: "I'm really liking the vibe in our new neighborhood!" (Sharing personal settling-in experiences).
  • Travelling Track ✈️: "We're loving this autumn view from our balcony!" (Real-time travel appreciation).

3. Language Proficiency Exams Strategy 📝

Exam Strategy Alert (IELTS / Cambridge / TOEFL):

  • Writing Exams (Task 2 / Essays): Maintain formal stative rules! Write "I thoroughly enjoy this initiative" (NOT "I'm loving this").
  • Speaking Exams (Part 1 & 2 Interview): Using "I'm loving..." or "I'm really enjoying..." demonstrates C1 natural idiomatic flow and emotional range!

4. Wrap-Up & CELTA CCQ Summary

CELTA CCQs: Does "I'm loving this coffee" mean a permanent state or an immediate experience? (An immediate live emotional experience right now).

3. Modern Dynamic Action Verbing: "Coffeeing" & "Machining" ☕⚙️

1. Meaning: Turning Nouns into Active Functional Verbs

Modern spoken English uses an ultra-productive pattern: turning any noun or machine into an active continuous verb ([Noun] is [Noun]-ing) to express whether an object, person, or brain is fulfilling its purpose.

2. Course Track Applications across Work, Housing & Travel

  • Professional Track 💼: "Work is working!" (The strategy is yielding great results) vs "Work is not working!" (Workflow failure).
  • Relocation Track 🏡: "The washing machine is not machining today." (Managing apartment appliance repairs).
  • Spoken 🗣️ & Travel ✈️ Tracks: "Coffee is coffee-ing today!" (The morning espresso has given great focus) vs "My brain is not braining today." (Playful admission of mental fatigue).

3. Language Proficiency Exams Strategy 📝

Exam Formal Register (IELTS / TOEFL) 📝 Spoken COSY Manner Upgrade 🗣️
"The coffee is producing a stimulating effect." "The coffee is coffee-ing!"
"The equipment is experiencing technical failure." "The machine is not machining."
"I am suffering from cognitive exhaustion." "My brain is not braining today."

4. Wrap-Up & CELTA CCQ Summary

CELTA CCQs: When someone says "My brain is not braining", is it literal or humorous? (Humorous admission of tiredness). Should you use this in an academic research paper? (No, strictly in informal spoken English).

4. Zero-Derivation Verb Shifting (Nouns into Active Verbs) 💻

1. Meaning: Zero-Derivation ("Verbing")

English converts well-known nouns directly into verbs without changing forms, creating concise, high-frequency active verbs.

2. Course Track Applications: Professional, Relocation & Travel

  • Professional Track 💼: "Let's calendar that client review" / "She bookmarked the quarterly report."
  • Relocation Track 🏡: "I spent the whole morning adulting — registering for utilities and local council taxes."
  • Travelling Track ✈️: "Just Uber to the city center, and I'll map our walking route."

3. Language Proficiency Exams Strategy 📝

High-Scoring Academic Noun-to-Verb Shifts for Exams:

  • to benchmark — "The study benchmarks international environmental standards."
  • to spearhead — "The government spearheaded a new educational initiative."
  • to bottleneck — "Supply chain delays bottlenecked economic recovery."

4. Wrap-Up & CELTA CCQ Summary

CELTA CCQs: Does verb shifting change the spelling of the noun? (No, zero-derivation keeps the exact form). Are terms like to benchmark accepted in formal C1 essays? (Yes, widely accepted in academic writing).

5. Modern Vibe Evaluators: "It's giving..." 🎃

1. Meaning: Aesthetic & Mood Evaluation

The popular spoken frame "It's giving..." functions as shorthand for "It radiates the aura, energy, or aesthetic of...".

2. Course Track Applications: Relocation, Travel & Spoken

  • Relocation Track 🏡: "This new apartment is giving warm, cozy family home."
  • Travelling Track ✈️: "This historic street in Prague is giving fairytale charm."
  • Spoken Track 🗣️: "Her autumn outfit is giving main character energy!"

3. Language Proficiency Exams Strategy 📝

Exam Formal Register (IELTS / Cambridge Essays) 📝 Spoken COSY Manner Upgrade 🗣️
"The architecture is evocative of nineteenth-century design." "It's giving nineteenth-century vibes."
"The atmosphere exudes tranquility and elegance." "It's giving luxury and peace."

4. Wrap-Up & CELTA CCQ Summary

CELTA CCQs: Does "It's giving luxury" mean someone gives you money? (No, it looks and feels high-end). What formal phrase replaces it in IELTS essays? ("It is evocative of..." or "It exudes...").
