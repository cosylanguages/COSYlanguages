My Top 5 Favourite English Grammar Rules for Intermediate Learners

Discover 5 dynamic and natural English grammar rules that native speakers use every day, from the flexible -ish suffix and emotional stative verbs to verb shifting and 'it's giving...'.
By JY DM • 📅 2026-10-15 • ⏱️ 6 min read • B1–B2 🎙️ EP. 10

Stepping into Shakespeare's Creative Laboratory 📜

At the intermediate level (CEFR B1–B2), true fluency begins when you embrace a creative mindset. Grammar is not a static list of rules to fear, but a dynamic canvas for self-expression. Here are my top 5 favourite rules for natural spoken English.

1. The Softening Magic of -ish (-like, -esque)

💡 CONVERSATIONAL MODEL

Let's meet at seven-ish near-ish to the central station.

Approximation softener: around 7:00 PM and close to the station.

Context: Casual scheduling with friends

2. Emotional Continuous: Stative Verbs on Stage

💡 CONVERSATIONAL MODEL

I'm really loving this autumn tea and I'm hating this rain today!

Heightened live emotion instead of permanent state.

Context: Expressing live feelings in spoken conversation

3. Action Verbing: Machine & Mind Purpose

💡 CONVERSATIONAL MODEL

The coffee is definitely coffee-ing, but my brain is not braining today.

Humorous expression of focus and mental energy.

Context: Workplace or study check-in

4. Zero-Derivation Verb Shifting

💡 CONVERSATIONAL MODEL

We are desk-lunching today, but let me benchmark this report first.

Using zero-derivation verbs for daily habits and C1 academic writing.

Context: Professional & daily routine

5. Modern Vibe Evaluators: 'It's Giving...'

💡 CONVERSATIONAL MODEL

This cozy autumn café is giving main character energy.

Aesthetic evaluation radiating a specific atmosphere or vibe.

Context: Describing atmosphere or aesthetic

Say It: Spoken Pronunciation Key 🗣️

seven-ish [/ˈsɛv.ən.ɪʃ/]
📌 Shakespeare's Creative Legacy

William Shakespeare coined over 1,700 English words by turning nouns into verbs and adding suffixes like -ish and -un. Intermediate learners continue this exact tradition in modern spoken English.

3-Question Mini-Quiz 📝

❓ What does 'meet at seven-ish' mean in spoken English?

The suffix -ish acts as a conversational softener indicating approximation.

❓ Which sentence uses emotional continuous for a stative verb?

'I'm loving' shifts the stative verb 'love' into continuous form to signal live heightened emotion.

❓ What formal phrase replaces 'It's giving nineteenth-century vibes' in academic writing?

'It is evocative of...' is the formal academic register equivalent of 'It's giving...'.
