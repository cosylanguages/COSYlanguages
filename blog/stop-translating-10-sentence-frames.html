Stop Translating! 10 Reusable Sentence Frames That Let You Speak From Day One

J By JY DM · 📅 Published September 14, 2026 · ⏱️ 8 min read
J

JY DM — Founder's Circumlocution & Podcast Deck

No-Translation Principles & Live Podcast Prompts

🗣️ CELTA Circumlocution Goal

Teach learners to describe unknown vocabulary using functional relative clauses ("person who...", "place where...", "thing that...") without stopping to translate.

🎙️ Podcast Host Cues

  • Demonstrate describing an object like "corkscrew" using "It's a thing you use to open bottles".
  • Emphasize that native speakers use circumlocution daily.
  • Challenge listeners to try describing 3 household items without naming them.

❓ Concept Checking Questions (CCQs)

When you forget a word, should you freeze or describe it? (Describe it!). Does "place where" describe people or locations? (Locations).

One of the biggest obstacles for beginner and intermediate language learners (CEFR A0–B1) is the habit of translating word by word. When you don't know an exact vocabulary item—like corkscrew, pharmacy, or architect—the brain often freezes and concludes: "I don't know this word, so I can't speak."

Our language learning methodology praises the elimination of translation in favor of the creative, natural approach used when we learn our first language. Fluent native speakers don't know every single word in their dictionary either; what they have is the ability to describe what they don't know. Circumlocution is a real communication skill, not a beginner trick.

Instead of memorizing isolated vocabulary lists, master these 10 reusable sentence frames and 20 survival phrases. Each frame acts like a LEGO piece: combine it with simple A0 vocabulary to create hundreds of original sentences and start thinking directly in English.

🛠️ The 10 Essential Sentence Frames

1. It's a...

Instead of stopping because you don't know the precise subcategory:

  • It's a book.
  • It's a problem.
  • It's a restaurant.
  • It's a game.

2. It's a person who...

Perfect for describing jobs or people when you forget the exact title. You don't need to recall teacher, doctor, driver, or chef immediately:

  • It's a person who teaches children. (Teacher)
  • It's a person who helps sick people. (Doctor / Nurse)
  • It's a person who drives a bus. (Driver)
  • It's a person who cooks in a restaurant. (Chef)

3. It's a place where...

One of the most versatile speaking patterns for locations:

  • It's a place where people buy food. (Supermarket / Market)
  • It's a place where children study. (School)
  • It's a place where you sleep. (Hotel / Bedroom)
  • It's a place where people exercise. (Gym)

4. It's a thing that...

Describe physical objects without searching frantically through a dictionary:

  • It's a thing that tells the time. (Clock / Watch)
  • It's a thing that opens doors. (Key)
  • It's a thing that keeps food cold. (Fridge)
  • It's a thing that you wear when it's cold. (Jacket / Coat)

5. It's something you use to...

Ideal when you know what an item does, but not its name:

  • It's something you use to cut bread. (Knife)
  • It's something you use to write. (Pen / Pencil)
  • It's something you use to clean the floor. (Broom / Vacuum)
  • It's something you use to carry water. (Bottle)

6. It's a time when...

Describe routines and moments effortlessly:

  • It's a time when people sleep. (Nighttime)
  • It's a time when children go to school. (Morning)
  • It's a time when families eat together. (Dinner time)
  • It's a time when people usually work. (Workday)

7. It's a day when...

Useful for holidays, anniversaries, and special events:

  • It's a day when families meet. (Holiday)
  • It's a day when people give presents. (Birthday / Christmas)
  • It's a day when many shops are closed. (Sunday)
  • It's a day when people celebrate. (Festival)

8. For example...

A simple connector that immediately makes your speech sound natural:

  • I like sports. For example, football and tennis.
  • I like fruit. For example, apples and bananas.
  • I study languages. For example, French and Italian.

9. It means...

Explain vocabulary using simple, clear words:

  • "Huge" means very big.
  • "Tiny" means very small.
  • "Borrow" means take something and give it back later.
  • "Quiet" means not noisy.

10. How do you say... in English?

The ultimate survival frame. It keeps the conversation alive in English instead of switching back to your native language:

  • How do you say this in English?
  • How do you say "..."?
  • Can you say it again?
  • What does it mean?

🎨 Communication Builders: Sensory & Descriptive Anchors

When an object or concept is harder to define by category, anchor your explanation using these sensory frames:

  • It's like... → It's like a big dog. • It's like a small computer. • It's like pizza, but sweeter.
  • It's for... → It's for cooking. • It's for children. • It's for travelling.
  • You use it to... → You use it to open bottles. • You use it to clean windows. • You use it to carry things.
  • It looks like... → It looks like a bird. • It looks like my brother. • It looks old.
  • It sounds... → It sounds interesting. • It sounds difficult. • It sounds expensive.
  • It feels... → It feels cold. • It feels soft. • It feels heavy.
  • It has... → It has four wheels. • It has long hair. • It has two doors.
  • It comes from... → It comes from Italy. • It comes from the sea. • It comes from plants.
  • It's made of... → It's made of wood. • It's made of metal. • It's made of plastic.
  • It helps... → It helps people. • It helps me study. • It helps you sleep.

🚀 20 Survival Phrases When You Forget a Word

Bookmark these 20 expressions to ensure you never get stuck during a conversation:

  1. It's a kind of... (It's a kind of animal.)
  2. It's a thing that... (It's a thing that you use in the kitchen.)
  3. It's something that... (It's something that helps you learn.)
  4. It's a person who... (It's a person who works in a hospital.)
  5. It's a place where... (It's a place where you buy medicine.)
  6. It's a time when... (It's a time when people celebrate.)
  7. It's like... (It's like a small computer.)
  8. It looks like... (It looks like a phone.)
  9. It sounds like... (It sounds like music.)
  10. It has... (It has four legs and a long tail.)
  11. You use it to... (You use it to cut food.)
  12. You can use it for... (You can use it for travelling.)
  13. It is used for... (It is used for transport.)
  14. It is made of... (It is made of wood or plastic.)
  15. It is similar to... (It is similar to a bicycle.)
  16. It is the opposite of... (It is the opposite of expensive.)
  17. It means... (It means the same as "begin".)
  18. It is the same as... (It is the same as "start".)
  19. I don't know the word, but... (I don't know the word, but it's something you use for cooking.)
  20. How do you say...? (How do you say this word in English?)

🌐 Multilingual Summary for FR, RU, IT & EL Learners

Sentence frames work universally across languages. Here is how these core frames translate into our target languages:

🇫🇷 French (Structures de phrases)

  • C'est une personne qui... (It's a person who...)
  • C'est un endroit où... (It's a place where...)
  • C'est quelque chose qu'on utilise pour... (It's something you use to...)
  • Comment dit-on... en français ? (How do you say... in French?)

🇷🇺 Russian (Конструкции предложений)

  • Это человек, который... (It's a person who...)
  • Это место, где... (It's a place where...)
  • Это вещь, которую используют для... (It's a thing used for...)
  • Как сказать... по-русски? (How do you say... in Russian?)

🇮🇹 Italian (Strutture di frasi)

  • È una persona che... (It's a person who...)
  • È un posto dove... (It's a place where...)
  • È qualcosa che si usa per... (It's something used to...)
  • Come si dice... in italiano? (How do you say... in Italian?)

🇬🇷 Greek (Δομές προτάσεων)

  • Είναι ένα άτομο που... (It's a person who...)
  • Είναι ένα μέρος όπου... (It's a place where...)
  • Είναι κάτι που χρησιμοποιείς για... (It's something you use to...)
  • Πώς λέγεται... στα ελληνικά; (How do you say... in Greek?)
