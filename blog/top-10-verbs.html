01

🧍 To Be

The very first verb every beginner needs — and the one where languages disagree the most.

The trap: English uses to be for age, hunger and thirst (“I am 25”, “I am hungry”). Most other COSY languages don't. Watch what each language really does:

  • Age uses HAVE, not BE, in French, Italian, Spanish and Portuguese — « j'ai 25 ans », « ho 25 anni », « tengo 25 años », « tenho 25 anos » (literally “I have 25 years”).
  • Age uses BE in German and Greek, just like English — „Ich bin 25 Jahre alt“, « Είμαι 25 χρονών ».
  • Age uses neither in Russian: «Мне 25 лет» literally means “to-me 25 years” — there is no verb at all in the present tense sentence.
  • Hunger/thirst use HAVE in French, Italian, Spanish, Portuguese and German — « j'ai faim », « ho fame », « tengo hambre », « tenho fome », „Ich habe Hunger“.
  • Greek has no BE or HAVE for hunger/thirst — it uses two single dedicated verbs: πεινάω (to-be-hungry) and διψάω (to-be-thirsty).
  • Russian usually drops “to be” entirely in the present tense — «Я Анна» (“I Anna”) is a complete, correct sentence.

Always double-check the age / hunger / thirst pattern per language before teaching it as a direct translation of English — it is the single biggest false-friend trap at A0.
